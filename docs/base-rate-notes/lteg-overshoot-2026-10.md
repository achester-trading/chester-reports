# Base-rate note: long-term growth estimates overshoot at peaks (2000, 2021)

**Why it exists.** F3 makes PEG and long-term expected EPS growth (LTEG)
**listed gaps**: no keyless source serves either, so neither prints and the flag
the order asked for does not exist. What the order wanted the pair for is the
caveat, that a low PEG is low because its denominator is inflated, and that

source; see the LTEG overshoot note".

## What the studies say about long-term growth estimates

1. **Analysts' long-term growth forecasts are too optimistic and predict little.**
   Across a broad cross-section of US stocks, there is no persistence in
   long-term earnings growth beyond chance, and IBES long-term growth forecasts
   "are overly optimistic and add little predictive power".
   Source: "The Level and Persistence of Growth Rates", *The Journal of
   Finance* 58 (2003),
   https://doi.org/10.1111/1540-6261.00540

2. **The error is systematic enough to trade.** Strategies that bet against
   analysts' long-term growth expectations earn superior returns "because
   expectations about future growth in earnings are too extreme".
   Source: "Expectations and the Cross-Section of Stock Returns",
   *The Journal of Finance* 51 (1996),
   https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1996.tb05223.x

3. **At the index level, high aggregate LTEG marks overreaction and is followed
   by disappointment.** An index of long-term expected earnings growth for S&P
   500 firms jointly predicts the errors in those expectations and the market's
   subsequent returns: good news makes expectations too optimistic, and low
   returns follow when they are disappointed.
   Source: "Belief Overreaction and Stock Market Puzzles", *Journal of
   Political Economy* (2024),
   https://doi.org/10.1086/727713 (working paper: NBER w27283,
   https://www.nber.org/papers/w27283)

## The two peaks

- **2000.** The S&P 500's expected long-term earnings growth peaked at 18.6% in
  the 2000 tech bubble, and had risen to 20.2% in the week of 5 May 2026, above
  that peak; since 1985 analysts have forecast about 13% a year against actual
  growth of about 7%. These figures are the source's own, which credits them to
  the independent research firm's series F3 refused as a source; they are
  quoted here as a dated citation, not read from a stored series.
  Source: Acadian Asset Management, "A pessimistic take on optimistic growth
  forecasts" (investment insights, June 2026),
  https://www.acadian-asset.com/investment-insights/owenomics/a-pessimistic-take-on-optimistic-growth-forecasts

- **2021.** **Not written.** No published study located on 23 Oct 2026 states
  the level of aggregate LTEG at the 2021 peak or its realised overshoot, and a
  figure without a citation is not written. The general result (items 1 to 3)
  applies to 2021 as to any high-LTEG episode; the episode's own numbers wait
  for a citable source.

## What this means for "cheap on PEG"

A PEG of forward P/E over LTEG falls when LTEG rises. On the studies above, a
high aggregate LTEG is the reading most likely to be revised down and to be
followed by low returns, so a record-low PEG produced by a record-high LTEG is
a measure of estimate optimism rather than of value. That is the caveat the
narrative register's proposal "the market is cheap on PEG" carries
(`config/narratives.yaml`, `market_cheap_on_peg`). The adjudicating series, the
realised forecast error (item 60), is itself a listed gap until a keyless
forward-EPS source with vintages is named.
