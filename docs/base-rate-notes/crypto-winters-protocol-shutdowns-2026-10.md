# Base-rate note: crypto shutdowns against the 2018-19 and 2022-23 winters

*Signal-triage Batch 15, item 78 (written as 71; ST-R6.78a's note, G3). Written
23 Oct 2026 by session B. Every claim below carries its source's title and URL
(no person is named: the repository names none); a figure is written only where
the cited source states it, and a comparison no source supports is said to be
missing. One-off: the note is not updated on a schedule.*

**Why it exists.** Item 78 asks how 2026's protocol shutdowns compare with the
last two crypto winters. The automated measure, DefiLlama's active-protocol
count, starts on the day of its first pull (DefiLlama serves today's list only),
so it has no winter to compare against yet. This note is the base rate in the
meantime, and its main finding is that the past counts are not like for like.

## The three episodes

- **2018-19.** Two sites cataloguing failed crypto projects had counted more than
  1,000 failed projects by the end of June 2018. Their "failed" is a token or
  coin abandoned, exposed as a scam or gone offline, not a protocol winding
  down: a count of dead tokens from the ICO boom.
  Source: TechCrunch, "Thousands of cryptocurrency projects are already dead"
  (29 Jun 2018),
  https://techcrunch.com/2018/06/29/thousands-of-cryptocurrency-projects-are-already-dead/

- **2022-23.** The winter's measured damage is in value, not in a count. The
  total value locked in DeFi protocols fell from about $180.7 billion at the end
  of 2021 to about $42.7 billion at the start of December 2022, on DefiLlama's
  data. **No published count of protocol shutdowns for 2022-23 was found** on 23
  Oct 2026, so none is written.
  Source: crypto.news, "Crypto yields and value locked sharply fall: FTX's
  collapse damaged DeFi" (1 Dec 2022),
  https://crypto.news/crypto-yields-and-value-locked-sharply-fall-ftxs-collapse-damaged-defi/

- **2026.** A researcher's compiled list counts 82 digital-asset projects and
  protocols that reportedly shut down in 2026, across DeFi, infrastructure and
  Web3 gaming; DefiLlama's own research in August had counted 100 projects that
  announced shutdowns in 2026, 40 of them in the preceding 90 days. The two lists
  use different criteria and are not added together.
  Source: Cointelegraph, "Crypto's 2026 shutdown wave claims 82 projects. What
  went wrong?" (20 Oct 2026),
  https://cointelegraph.com/news/crypto-project-shutdowns-2026-what-went-wrong

## What the base rate says

The three counts measure different things: dead tokens in 2018, value lost in
2022, announced protocol shutdowns in 2026. So the comparison the order asks for
can only be made in kind, not in number. 2018's failures were mostly tokens with
no working product; 2022's damage came through the collapse of centralised
lenders and exchanges and the fall in locked value; 2026's list is of protocols
and infrastructure with users and revenue winding down. That last is the
supply-side breadth damage item 78 is about. Whether it is worse than 2022's is
not established by any cited count.

The measure that can settle it from here on is the stored one: DefiLlama's
active-protocol count, daily from its first pull, in the Weekly addendum's
incidents section and the Monthly's digital chapter. The monthly hand count from
compiled lists is a listed gap (G3).
