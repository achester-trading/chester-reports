## 1. The read

The quarter closed with the S&P 500 at 7,652 — +12.7% YTD, +2.3% on the quarter, 27 all-time highs so far this year — and the 10-year at 5.29%. That pairing is the whole document. Forward earnings did all the work and more: the index de-rated from roughly 22x forward on 1 January to 19.0x on 30 September while 2026 EPS growth runs at +31.6% (margin expansion contributing 19.5 points, revenue 13.2, share count −1.1) and consensus carries $362 → $417 → $488 for 2026–28. JPM leads with the 19.0x, which is only +1.1x over the 30-year average of 17.2x and inside one standard deviation. The numbers it does not lead with: CAPE 40.9x against a 28.9x 30-year average, and the forward earnings yield minus the Baa corporate yield at −1.0% against a +0.7% average. The earnings yield (≈5.3%) now equals the 10-year and sits a point below Baa. The 2000 top had a 25x multiple on a 6.2% 10-year; this market has a 19x multiple on a 5.3% 10-year with a hiking cycle priced.

Rates did the de-rating. The curve shifted up 100–140bp YTD and bear-flattened: 2y 3.47% → 4.88%, 10y 4.18% → 5.29%, 30y 4.84% → 5.64%, 20y 5.7%. The Agg yields 5.56% (real 3.12%, above its 2.48% long-run average); the 10-year is −5.3% YTD in total return, the 30-year −8.1%, IG −3.1%, munis −4.2%; the only fixed-income winners were loans (+3.5%), converts (+14.7%), ABS and the 2-year. Fed funds sit near 3.9%; the September dots carry 4.10% for both end-2026 and end-2027 (long run 3.20%), while the OIS curve carries roughly 4.3% for end-2026 and 4.9% through 2027 — the market prices two to three more hikes than the committee. The FOMC's own 2026 forecast (headline PCE 3.7%, core 3.4%, 4Q/4Q) sits above the August prints (3.4%, 3.0%): the Fed expects inflation to rise into year-end, and still projects only one hike.

Growth is hot, not slowing. 2Q GDP +2.2% with consumption +2.5pp and business fixed investment +1.3pp (AI capex) against net exports −1.1pp and inventories −0.5pp; the September composite PMI is 58.4 for the U.S. and 54.9 globally, services 55.3, manufacturing 53.0 — a synchronized acceleration. Labor is the soft spot: August payrolls +162K but the 3-month average is 71K, unemployment 4.1%, wage growth 3.3%, and the foreign-born share of the labor force is down a full point from its March 2025 peak (19.7% → 18.7%). Headline CPI 3.4% (May peak 4.2%), core CPI 2.4%, headline PCE 3.4%, core PCE 3.0%. WTI $90 after a $55 (16 Dec) → $113 (7 Apr) → $90 round trip. Consumer sentiment 48.1 — below the June 2022 trough, against a record-high index. The K-shape is the reconciling fact: top-20% net worth +$58tn since 4Q19 vs +$19tn for the bottom 80%; the top quintile earns 51% of pre-tax income and does 38% of the spending.

Leadership rotated inside the AI complex, not away from it. JPM's five AI buckets are 53.2% of the index (semis 18.6%, hyperscalers 17.8%, hardware 11.2%, software 9.3%, power 2.5%); YTD semis +44%, hardware +37%, power +22%, hyperscalers +7%, software +4%. Mag 7, S&P 493 and S&P 500 are all +12% YTD — the Mag 7's share of index return fell to 34% from 46–63% in 2023–25. Micron and AMD are now top-10 constituents; the top 10 are 40.6% of market cap on 36.1% of earnings, at 20.9x — exactly their own long-run average — while the other 490 trade at 17.9x, 112% of theirs. Value beat growth in every cap tier (large value +19.3% vs large growth +6.3%; small value +17.0% vs small growth +10.7%), energy +40%, commodities the top asset class at +33%, Korea +98% and Taiwan +71% in dollars; China −12%, India −15%. Hyperscaler capex: $416bn (2025) → $799bn (2026F, +92%) → $1,093bn (2027F) → $1,200bn (2028F); free cash flow is heading to zero and the gap is going to the IG market, where the five hyperscalers are 3.5% of the index at net debt/EBITDA of 1.3x vs a 2.7x IG median. JPM Global Research puts the 2026–30 AI buildout at $5.5tn of funding.

Credit is the complacent corner. IG OAS 80bp — the 5th percentile of 25 years — and HY 311bp (17th), while all-in yields sit at the 88th (IG, 6.0%) and 69th (HY, 8.3%) percentiles. Treasuries did the work; spreads have not priced the hiking cycle. HY is higher quality than 2007 (BB 54% vs 32%, CCC 7% vs 15%); the CCC-type risk migrated to private credit, now ~$2.5tn of AUM and off every spread index.

Cross-timeframe: the quarterly bar is +2.3% with a −9% maximum drawdown YTD; the monthly bars show the index grinding to new highs while the 10-year added ~110bp — a multi-month regime in which equities shrugged off the rate move because earnings beat the de-rating. The question for 4Q is whether 2027 EPS (+15%) survives the curve repricing the terminal rate toward 5%.

## 2. What doesn't fit

- **Sentiment 48.1 at an all-time high.** JPM's base rate — nine sentiment troughs averaged +24.1% over the following twelve months vs +4.8% after peaks — was built on troughs that coincided with market lows (1974, 1980, 2008, 2022). A trough at a price high has no precedent in the sample. Logged as a disagreement with the base rate's applicability, not as a buy signal.
- **Core CPI 2.4% vs core PCE 3.0%.** Core PCE normally runs below core CPI (smaller shelter weight). The sign flipped: the gap is in the PCE-heavy services categories (health care, financial services, non-profits) and tariff-exposed goods. Worth a metric lens: the CPI–PCE core gap as a series.
- **The dots.** A 4.10% funds rate through 2027 against a 3.7% headline / 3.4% core PCE forecast for 2026 is a real policy rate near zero while inflation rises. The curve (4.9% through 2027) disagrees. Whichever is right, the forecast table itself is the hawkish document.
- **Concentration is not where the premium is.** Top-10 P/E at 100% of its own average, the remaining 490 at 112%. The 2021 configuration (mega-caps expensive, rest cheap) has inverted. The concentration risk is earnings-path risk in semis, not a multiple.
- **International outperformed with a firmer dollar** (DXY +3% YTD). JPM's own slide 44 says international outperformance comes in declining-dollar periods. The driver this year was semiconductor earnings (Korea, Taiwan), not currency — a sector trade wearing a regional label.
- **The de-rating is fully a rates story.** Forward P/E 22x → 19x while EY–Baa stayed negative: multiples compressed exactly as much as the discount rate rose and not a point more. No risk aversion has been priced.
- **Oil at $90 with U.S. net petroleum imports at −0.6% of GDP.** The U.S. is a net exporter; the April spike was a price shock without a balance-of-payments shock, which is why the dollar firmed through it.

## 3. Where our read lands

The Disruptive Themes composite ran at −1.2 (OVEREXTENDED) in August with CAPE near 40–41; JPM's 40.9x confirms the level. But three of JPM's exhibits argue the vulnerability is elsewhere than the composite's valuation leg: the multiple has already compressed, the top 10 trade at their own average, and HY quality is higher than at the last two tops. The hot legs are rates (at prior tops the 10-year was 6.2% in 2000, 4.7% in 2007, 1.6% in 2020 and 2022 — 5.3% now is 2000-class), the earnings path (consensus +31.6% / +15% / +17% for 2026–28, with margins at 15.9% doing most of 2026), and credit complacency (IG 5th percentile). For the Top & Bottom report, the inflection-points table (slide 4) should become a standing exhibit; for the Bull Rebuttal gate, the standing rebuttal is now "earnings +31%, PMI 58, mega-cap multiple at average."

## 5. Quarterly mechanism

JPM publishes the U.S. GTM at the same URL within the first business days of January, April, July and October, data as of the prior quarter-end. A scheduled task ("JPM Guide to the Markets — quarterly read") fires on the 6th of those months at about 7 AM ET, cloud-only, web sources only, and delivers `docs/scans/jpm-gtm-YYYYqN.md` in this shape: the read; deltas against the prior edition (the reference table in §6); what doesn't fit; candidates in the intake template with proposed rulings; a voices entry; a refreshed reference table. A ≤150-word chat summary follows. Rulings are taken in chat; adopted items join that month's change-order batch; the figures feed the next Monthly as sourced figures, the recommendations do not. The task's prompt carries the 4Q26 reference table as its baseline; after each run, replace the baseline in the task with the refreshed table (one line to a chat session).
