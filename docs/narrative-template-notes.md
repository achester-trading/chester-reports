# The secondary notes' paragraph: style and coverage

*The style contract for the open note (09:50) and the evening note (21:30), read by `daily_cascade/note_common.prose` as the guide. Ruled by the secondary-notes order, 19 Oct 2026. The system prompt carries the absolute rules: every number from the payload, no recommendation, one paragraph.*

## When a paragraph is written

**Only when the note's gate opens.**
- **The open note's gate:** a tier-1 08:30 release; a gap or first-fifteen-minute range beyond the gauge's implied one-session move; a volatile-day trigger the 07:00 anchor printed.
- **The evening note's gate:**
  - on weekdays: an earnings night for a captured name; ES or NQ past its threshold; dollar-yen or an Asia index past its threshold; a yen or devaluation hook tripped;
  - always on Sunday.

On any other day every line is code-written and no model is called.

## Length

- **The open note:** at most 250 words.
- **The evening note:** at most 150 words on a weekday and 400 on Sunday.
- **Over the cap:** a reply over its cap is withheld with its count, never trimmed.

## What the paragraph does

- **It leads with the most material block,** and says what the lines add up to rather than repeating each figure. The lines are printed beneath it.
- **The open note:** the releases first on a release day, then the market's reaction.
  - There is no consensus for any macro release. If you cite a print, say so. The payload's `naive_surprise` is a surprise against a declared naive expectation and must be called "naive".
  - The open against the gauge and the levels follows. The levels are our own engine's, under the convention printed in the block.
- **The evening note:** the futures since the 16:00 close, any after-hours print against its implied move, Asia's open, currencies, crypto and the news.
- **On Sunday:** the futures open against Friday first, then the weekend's news, with what is new since the 05:00 Weekly first. For each item, say whether the futures open is consistent with it, as a description and never as a cause.

## What it never does

- **Never a cause.** A move is consistent or inconsistent with an item; it is never caused by it ("attributions, not causes").
- **Never anything new or acted on.** No recommendation and no suggested action. No new flag, tell, packet or Book C draft.
- **Never a second regime.** The regime is the one the 07:00 anchor or the 16:45 close stamped.
- **Never a vendor level.** No vendor's levels, and no figure from a third-party paid report.
