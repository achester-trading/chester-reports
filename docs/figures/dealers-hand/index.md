| 2 | Value before expiry: intrinsic + extrinsic. The smooth curve is the put’s price today; the kinked line is what it would be worth if exercised now. | 5 | Theta: the earned-premium schedule. The at-the-money put’s extrinsic value against days remaining. | 6 | The two worlds: the same shocks, two regimes. Identical random shocks fed through the two hedging regimes of Chapter 4. | 8 | Vanna: the hedge shrinks when vol falls. A dealer short one OTM put hedges its −0.25 delta by shorting 25 index-units. The event passes, implied vol crushes from 20 to 15, and the same put’s delta shrinks to −0.18 — so the dealer buys back 7 units. Multiply across the whole protective-put book and the vol crush itself produces the post-event bid, before any news is “priced in.” | [`fig-08.svg`](fig-08.svg) |
| 9 | The 0DTE delta cliff. Call delta across spot for the same 7,500 strike at three points on the clock. This is Figure 4’s gamma spike seen one derivative down, and it is what “pin risk” means mechanically. | [`fig-09.svg`](fig-09.svg) |
| 10 | The monthly gamma cycle. | 19 | The six session types by 0DTE signature. Stylized intraday paths. What differs across them is not the news — pin days and trend days can share a headline calendar — but whether the 0DTE book built balanced or lopsided, and whether the walls held on first test or migrated with price. | 20 | A nested-flip day, worked. Configuration 3 as a session: a routine morning drift crosses the 0DTE flip at 7,468 and the decline promptly steepens — the intraday book is now hedging pro-cyclically — until the weekly structure at 7,450/7,440 absorbs it. The recovery re-crosses the 0DTE flip and the grind resumes. The monthly flip at 7,403 was never at risk; the regime never changed; only the intraday physics did, for ninety minutes. Trading the 0DTE flip as a stop level and the monthly flip as the invalidation — Chapter 11.3’s rule — is exactly the difference between a managed trade and a panic. | [`fig-20.svg`](fig-20.svg) |
| 21 | Flip-stack width — the fragility gauge. The same net GEX headline can sit on either of these ladders. On the left, a decline meets three flips in sequence and each hand-off decelerates it.
