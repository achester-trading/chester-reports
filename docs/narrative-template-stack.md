# Narrative template — the stacked reports (daily close and Weekly)

The style guide each section's prose is written against (`daily_cascade/stack_prose.py`). Tone, structure and density only; the section's own data is the only source of figures. Replaces, for the stacked editions, the close template's reference paragraph, which ended on what the system tested and what it will test next — a closer the stack does not use (T2.1, 4 Oct 2026).

## Register

A desk's register. Short declarative sentences, one idea per paragraph, the figure inside the sentence rather than in a parenthesis after it. Name the mechanism, not the mood: "dealers must sell X per 1% decline", not "the market feels heavy".

## Shape (T2.5, ruled 5 Oct 2026)

Every section of the daily close, the Weekly and the Monthly prints in one fixed order: the claim line, the table(s), the chart(s), **one paragraph of at most 120 words**, the footnote. The tables carry every figure; the paragraph cites **at most six** of them and says what they mean together, never re-listing a row. The read is one paragraph of at most five sentences whose first sentence is the email's subject; it never copies a section's sentence, because a sentence printed twice is withheld where it repeats. A section with no new facts prints its footnote alone. Nothing prints as a bullet list.

## What a section does

It says what happened in its own window and stops. It does not end on what the next session or week will test, and it does not write about the system, the report, its data or its feeds. **Only the Ahead section looks forward**, and it does so with the calendar, the scenarios and the base rates it is given.

## What a section never does

- Narrate an absence. A series not yet tracked is printed in the section's footnote by the report; the prose does not mention it.
- Write about the method. No sentence about how the report, its checks or its data work ("for the system", "which is itself the finding", "written as such rather than left silent", "a count is attention, not evidence", "cited by id", "said positively so"); the glossary and the Reader's Guide carry that.
- Name an internal id. Contradictions, exceptions, metrics and sources are written in the plain names the data gives.
- Recommend. No buy, sell, add, trim, hold-as-advice, or any synonym.
