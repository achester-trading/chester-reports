# PART 0 — FOUNDATIONS

## 0.1 The listed tail is not the black swan

The source list opens with the correct disclaimer and it deserves to be the first substantive argument of this paper rather than a footnote: **anything on a list is priceable by definition; the genuine tail is absent from any list that could be written.** Taleb's distinction is between the unknown unknown and the merely improbable, and every item in the twenty-five belongs firmly in the second category. Each has a named mechanism, an identifiable first-mover series, and in most cases a historical instance. That makes them *high-impact, underpriced* risks, which is a useful and entirely different thing from black swans.

Why bother, then, if the list cannot contain the event that matters?

Because the value of the list is not prediction. It is three other things. First, **language discipline**: when a tripwire fires, the report's characterization of the environment changes in a pre-specified way, which prevents the far more common failure of narrating a shock after the fact with the confidence of having anticipated it. Second, **instrumentation**: the exercise of assigning a first-mover series to each scenario forces the question of what is actually being watched, and the answer is frequently "nothing," which is information. Third, and most valuable, **the mutation space**: the scenarios as written will not recur in the form written, but their *mechanisms* recur constantly in new venues, wearing new instruments, carried by new actors. A framework that has thought carefully about how a funding accident works is better prepared for the funding accident that arrives through a channel nobody listed, because the physics are portable even when the plumbing is not.

That last point is why this edition adds a Mutations element to every scenario. The original list's own design principle 6 states that scenarios get re-specified as the world changes, and offers scenario 7 as the example: it was scoped as a chokepoint *closure* risk, the closure occurred, and the scenario was rewritten as the divergence between physical disruption and price. That re-specification was correct and it was *reactive*. The Mutations element exists to make the same work anticipatory: to map the ways a scenario might outgrow its own scoping before the world forces the rewrite.

The honest limit of the whole exercise: this widens the lit area around each item. It does not illuminate the dark.

## 0.2 The n≈0 problem, and what it forbids

Every one of these scenarios has an effective sample size near zero. A few have one clean historical instance (the 2019 repo episode, the 2022 gilt crisis, Reserve Primary in 2008). Several have one loose analog in a different institutional setting. Some have none at all, because the structure that would fail has not previously existed at systemic scale: the price-insensitive buyer class, in particular, is instrumented against a market structure that is roughly fifteen years old.

Three prohibitions follow, and they are the discipline that keeps this paper from becoming its own hazard.

**No backtesting, and no implied precision.** There is no honest way to compute a hit rate, a lead time, or an optimal threshold for a series that has fired once. Any threshold in this paper is a reading aid, not a calibrated parameter. Where the source list assigns cadence tiers, those tiers reflect the *update frequency of the underlying series*, not a confidence ranking.

**No position sizing off a tripwire.** With n near zero, treating a firing as a trade signal is the most severe overfitting available: it fits a rule to a single observation and then risks capital on it. The correct output of a firing is a change in analytical language, a tightening of a related predicate elsewhere in the system, and in some cases an instruction to look harder at a second series. Nothing more.

**No aggregation into a score.** These do not sum. Twenty-five scenarios at low probability do not compose into a single "tail index," because their correlations are unknown, structurally unstable, and in stress overwhelmingly positive. The framework's separation of the composite (which scores conditions) from the tail list (which conditions language) is the correct architecture, and collapsing the second into the first would recreate exactly the false-precision failure the composite's integer scoring was designed to prevent.

What remains after those prohibitions is still substantial: a map of mechanisms, a set of instruments, and a discipline of noticing.

## 0.3 The price-insensitive buyer: the paper's central claim

Scenarios 21 through 25 are grouped in the source list as a distinct class, described as arguably more dangerous than the shocks that precede them. That judgment is correct and it is the most important analytical claim in the entire tail architecture, so it is argued here rather than asserted.

**The mechanism.** A price-insensitive buyer purchases an asset for reasons unrelated to its price: a passive fund buys because it received a contribution, an investment-grade mandate buys because the bond is rated BBB− or better, a stablecoin issuer buys bills because statute requires it, a sovereign fund buys because oil revenue exceeded the domestic budget, an ETF authorized participant creates because the arbitrage is open. None of them is expressing a view. All of them are executing a rule.

**Why the withdrawal is worse than the arrival was good.** Price-insensitive buying is stabilizing on the way up, and its *absence* is destabilizing in a way that ordinary selling is not, for three reasons. It does not respond to cheapness, so the usual stabilizer — value buyers arriving as prices fall — does not engage against it. It is mechanical, so it does not pause to reconsider. And it is frequently *forced*, which means it accelerates precisely as conditions deteriorate: the downgrade wave grows as spreads widen, the redemption grows as the discount grows, the collateral call grows as the collateral falls.

**Why standard macro indicators are silent.** This is the part that makes the class dangerous rather than merely unpleasant. Nothing in an unemployment rate, a PMI, a yield curve, or a credit spread tells you that half of investment grade sits at the BBB boundary against mandates that cannot hold high yield. Nothing in a CPI print tells you that retirement decumulation has crossed contribution flows. The instruments required are structural and mostly non-macro: ratings distributions, fund flow data, reserve attestations, creation-redemption activity, premium and discount to net asset value. A macro dashboard, however good, cannot see any of it. The framework's own ten-pillar Monthly is a macro dashboard.

**Why now.** Each of the five members of this class is larger than at any prior point, and three of them did not meaningfully exist in their current form before 2010: passive equity ownership at its present share, the BBB tier at its present share of investment grade, and stablecoin issuers holding bills at a scale that appears in sovereign financing plans. The structures are new, the withdrawals are untested, and the instrumentation is thin.

The paper's recommendation, stated once here and revisited in the coverage audit: **if the build queue can only address one family, it should be this one.**

## 0.4 Cadence follows speed, not importance

The source list's first design principle is easy to misread as a ranking, so it is worth restating precisely. Taiwan (scenario 8) is plausibly the highest-impact item on the list and is checked *quarterly*. A funding-market accident (scenario 1) is checked *daily*. This is not a claim that repo plumbing matters more than a Taiwan conflict. It is a claim about the **update frequency of the first-mover series**.

Taiwan's honest instruments — export orders, foundry revenue, war-risk quotations, exercise tempo — move on monthly-to-quarterly clocks. Checking them daily produces noise dressed as vigilance, and worse, it manufactures a *feeling* of monitoring that displaces the real work. The SOFR–IORB spread, by contrast, moves intraday and can go from unremarkable to alarming inside one session, which is why it earns a daily slot despite instrumenting a scenario of far lower ultimate consequence.

The corollary matters for how this paper should be read: **a quarterly scenario is not a background scenario.** Tier D contains the two items on the list whose realization would most thoroughly reorder the world. Their cadence reflects the speed at which evidence about them arrives, nothing else.

## 0.5 The seven-element template

Each scenario below runs the same sequence.

**Mechanism.** How it breaks, step by step, with the actual counterparties named. Not "credit stress spreads" but who owns what, who must sell, and to whom.

**Precedent.** The closest historical instance and, more importantly, the specific respects in which today differs. Precedents in this domain are more often disanalogies than templates.

**Tripwire.** The first-mover series from the source list, what a firing looks like in numbers where a number is meaningful, and an explicit statement of what the firing does *not* mean. Per the source's design principle 2, a scenario without a tripwire is commentary. Scenario 13 is the deliberate exception, and its section explains why.

**Current state.** Dormant, loading, or armed, as of August 29, 2026. *Dormant*: mechanism intact, no elevated pressure. *Loading*: preconditions accumulating. *Armed*: the structure is in place and only a trigger is missing.

**Transmission.** Which of the five Disruptive Themes factors the scenario lands on, and the second-order path from there. This is what connects the tail architecture to the composite.

**Mutations.** Four bounded questions, per the discipline agreed for this edition: *scope drift* (same mechanism, different scale or venue), *substitution* (same failure, different instrument or actor), *aliasing* (which other scenarios it becomes indistinguishable from once underway), and the *re-specification trigger* (the condition under which this stops being a tail and becomes a current condition requiring a rewrite, as happened to scenario 7). Two to four mutations per scenario, each with a named mechanism and either a first-mover series or an explicit admission that none exists. The same test applies here as to the parent scenario: without an instrument or an acknowledged gap, it is adjacency, not analysis.

**Report routing.** Which of the five reports carries the scenario, at what cadence, and what a firing changes in that report's language. Where no report currently carries the required series, the section says so, and the item goes to the build queue in Part 2.

## The clustering map — what fires together

The single most important operational fact in this paper: **these do not fire independently.** Six clusters account for most of the correlation structure.

**The plumbing cascade — 1 + 19 + 9 + 25.** One event with four names. A duration shock generates collateral calls, forced selling meets constrained dealer balance sheet, an auction clears badly into the stress, and the wrapper that promised liquidity discovers it cannot deliver. Any one firing should raise attention on the other three immediately. The framework will not be able to identify which one it is looking at, and should say so.

**The buildout cluster — 3 + 20 + 14 (data-center mutation) + 24 (AI-adjacent mutation).** A capex halt reprices the concentrated index, the infrastructure credit financed against contracted demand, and the investment-grade paper issued to build it. This cluster is the direct tail-side expression of the *Foundations* paper's Factors I and II, and its credit legs would move faster than its equity legs.

**The bill-market cluster — 4 + 23 + 9 + 1.** Stablecoin demand for bills is load-bearing in Treasury financing. Whether that demand fails (4), is removed by rule (23), or simply proves insufficient at auction (9), the consequence lands in a reserve system with no buffer (1). Four routes, one destination.

**The price-insensitive withdrawal cluster — 21 + 22 + 24 + 11.** All four are mechanical bids disappearing. In aggregate they would present as an unexplained persistent offer with no news attached, which is exactly the environment in which analysts reach for narrative explanations. The correct language when this cluster is suspected is structural, not sentimental.

**The physical cluster — 7 + 8 + 17 + 12.** Shipping, insurance and commodity repricing share a signature. A Taiwan escalation would initially be indistinguishable from a chokepoint event in market terms.

**The settlement cluster — 13 + 18 + 8.** Aliased in the opening hours by construction, as §Family F argues.

**The correlating variable across all six clusters is the long yield**, which the *Foundations* paper identifies as the common bus. Every cluster either originates in it, transmits through it, or is amplified by it.

# PART 3 — THE SECOND-ORDER SCAN: MUTATIONS BEYOND THE LIST

## Method — what the list's own structure cannot see

The Mutations elements in Part 1 asked, scenario by scenario: *how does this risk escape its own scoping?* This scan asks the prior question: **what does the list's structure itself prevent us from seeing?** A scenario list organized around mechanisms inherits the blind spots of its taxonomy — risks that cut *across* every family at once, risks in the system's own sensors, and risks in structures too new to have earned a scenario number. This is the tail-list version of the Broad Media Scan discipline in Disruptive Themes, which has already proven itself once: June's three blind spots all became framework components by August.

The scan below draws on the current official-sector literature — the FSB's 2024 AI report and its 2026 successor framework, the IMF's 2026 financial-stability work on AI and cyber, BIS work on the AI supply chain, UK Financial Policy Committee analysis — plus cross-domain reasoning of the kind no stability report will print. The discipline of Part 0 is retained without exception: every class below names a mechanism, an earliest observable or an acknowledged gap, and the scenarios it rewires. And the promotion discipline from Factor V applies: nothing here enters the tail list at full enthusiasm. Candidates go through the same quarantine.

One honest note on sourcing: several of these classes are now consensus concerns in the official literature, which by this framework's own crowding logic *reduces* their marginal value — a risk the FSB has a chapter on is at least partly priced into supervisory behavior. The classes flagged **[not in the literature]** are the ones this scan considers most valuable, precisely because nobody official is writing about them.

## M1 · Model monoculture — the analytical commons fails together

**Mechanism.** When many institutions run credit decisions, risk management, and trading on the same handful of foundation models trained on similar data with similar architectures, idiosyncratic model risk becomes correlated market risk. The failure is not any model being wrong; it is every model being wrong *the same way at the same moment* — synchronized credit contractions, parallel risk-off moves, simultaneous rebalancing. The IMF's 2026 work makes the specific point that future flash events may arise less from coding errors than from many AI systems reacting in parallel to the same signal, and IMF analysis finds AI-based funds already rebalance materially faster than traditional strategies. The FSB's own framework acknowledges herding and has been criticized, fairly, for not treating it as the central threat. The 1987 portfolio-insurance episode is the ancestor: a shared decision rule, executed simultaneously, converting a decline into a cascade. The difference is that portfolio insurance was one strategy sold to some institutions; the foundation-model layer is becoming the substrate of *analysis itself*.

**The self-referential clause, which belongs in this framework's own file:** this report system is AI-assisted. The five reports, the white papers, and this scan share a substrate with the analytical layer of the market they monitor. If the foundation-model commons has a correlated blind spot, this framework plausibly sits inside it. The existing countermeasures — the designated bull, the calibration panel, the WEF cross-check — are the right shape but are themselves increasingly AI-mediated at the source. The only structural defense is deliberate methodological diversity: at least one lens in the calibration panel should be primitive on purpose (pure price, pure positioning, no model).

**Earliest observable:** correlation spikes without news; simultaneous strategy-crowding unwinds of the August 2024 type at rising frequency. No direct instrument exists for adoption concentration. **Gap, acknowledged.**

**Rewires:** 20 (the mechanical seller becomes the analytical layer itself), 22, 1, and the Calibration Check discipline in Disruptive Themes.

## M2 · Agentic finance — runs at machine speed

**Mechanism.** The FSB's current framework flags agentic AI — systems that plan and execute multi-step tasks without continuous human direction — as a qualitatively different risk class: goal misalignment, emergent agent-to-agent behavior, and the impossibility of real-time human monitoring at scale. Fuse that with the payment rails of Factor V and the mutation sharpens: **autonomous agents holding balances and executing payments on stablecoin infrastructure remove the human latency that every run model assumes.** The 2023 regional-bank run compressed the classic multi-week run into hours because of mobile banking; an agentic run compresses hours into minutes, because thousands of agents parse the same headline and execute the same withdrawal logic with no coffee break and no doubt. The same applies to redemption waves in tokenized funds (scenario 4's most important mutation) and to wrapper arbitrage (25).

**Earliest observable:** agent-initiated payment share where rails publish it; agent-driven order flow statistics if exchanges disclose them. Neither exists today. **Gap, acknowledged.**

**Rewires:** 4, 23, 25, 1 — the entire bill-market cluster, at a speed setting the cluster's current instrumentation does not contemplate.

## M3 · The retirement perimeter breach — scenarios 6, 22 and 25 fuse **[promotion candidate]**

**Mechanism.** This is the most concrete new structure of 2026, and it is a fusion of three scenarios this paper treats separately. The August 2025 executive order directed the opening of defined-contribution plans to alternative assets; the Labor Department's March 30, 2026 proposed rule delivered a process-based safe harbor for fiduciaries adding private equity, private credit, real estate and digital assets to 401(k) menus — a perimeter shift affecting roughly 90 million participants and a $10-trillion-plus 401(k) pool, with target-date vehicles (now $5.3 trillion and growing fast in CIT form) as the realistic delivery mechanism.

Assemble the parts: **scenario 6's stale-mark problem** (quarterly, model-driven private valuations) placed inside **scenario 25's wrapper promise** (daily-priced default vehicles) held by **scenario 22's mechanical flows** (contribution-driven buying now, glide-path and decumulation selling later). Each scenario's Part 1 treatment assumed institutional holders who understand gates and can wait out marks. Retirement defaults change the holder to someone who never chose the asset, and change the politics of the first gated redemption from a fund dispute into a national event. Congressional scrutiny began within weeks — a May 2026 GAO request explicitly citing reports of private credit funds blocking redemptions and taking downgrades — which is itself evidence that the political fuse is already attached. The contingent-liability logic of scenario 4 applies in full: assets inside the retirement perimeter acquire an implicit sovereign backstop the moment enough voters hold them.

**The slow layer underneath:** the same rule change arrives precisely as the boomer decumulation turns the aggregate retirement system from accumulator to distributor — mechanical, calendar-driven selling meeting newly illiquid holdings.

**Earliest observables:** the DOL rule's finalization calendar and legal challenges; alternative-asset share in new target-date series; the first major provider to embed alternatives in a default vehicle; gated-redemption incidents in retail-distributed private funds. All are readable today; none is instrumented. **This class deserves promotion to a numbered scenario at the next Monthly revision — call it the leading candidate for scenario 26.**

**Rewires:** 6, 22, 25, and through the backstop logic, 4's moral-hazard branch.

## M4 · The legal-constitutional channel — partially realized, and now precedented

**Mechanism.** Courts as macro actors. The class was hypothetical until February 20, 2026, when the Supreme Court's 6–3 ruling in *Learning Resources v. Trump* held that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs — invalidating the largest tariff regime in modern history at a stroke. The fiscal mechanics that followed are a template for the class: roughly $160–175 billion of collections rendered refundable, some $81 billion already refunded this fiscal year against $5 billion in the prior-year period, the average effective tariff rate falling from roughly 16.8% toward 9% before partial replacement under other authorities, and the refund surge landing on a deficit already past $1.3 trillion for the fiscal year with interest costs across the trillion-dollar line. A judicial opinion moved nine figures of fiscal flow and repriced the entire trade-policy path in an afternoon — no market stress required, no macro data involved.

**The forward docket is the point.** The precedent generalizes to every policy-dependent revenue and rate assumption in the framework: challenges to replacement tariff authorities (Section 122's time limits, Section 232 scope), litigation over central-bank governance and removal powers — which is scenario 10 arriving through a courtroom rather than an announcement — and, further out, constitutional questions any TGA-funded operation of the Factor IV type would eventually attract. The mutation class also cuts the other way: rulings can *create* fiscal holes (refunds) or close policy options abruptly, in both directions, on a calendar nobody trades.

**Earliest observable:** the docket itself — a reading discipline exactly parallel to scenario 23's legislative calendar, and the same lesson applies: the cheapest instrument on the list is the easiest to neglect. **Rewires:** 10, 16, 9, and Factor IV's entire policy-path analysis.

## M5 · The statistical substrate — when the sensors decay **[not in the literature]**

**Mechanism.** Every scenario in this paper, every pillar in the Monthly, and every factor in Disruptive Themes runs on official statistics — the Monthly alone consumes dozens of government series. The mutation is not that the data fails visibly; it is that it **decays gradually while the dashboards keep rendering**: survey response rates have fallen for over a decade across the major household and establishment surveys, budget pressure shrinks samples and kills sub-indices, revision magnitudes grow, and — the 2025 escalation — the statistical agencies themselves became political terrain, with a BLS commissioner dismissed over a payroll revision. None of this produces a bad print with a flag on it. It produces *wider error bars that are not printed*, on the same page layout, at the same decimal precision, consumed by the same models (see M1) at the same confidence.

This is scenario 13's epistemics applied to the framework's own inputs: the failure mode is invisibility, and a decorative number is worse than an acknowledged gap. There is also a Goodhart layer — series that become policy battlegrounds (payrolls, CPI) acquire pressure toward flattering methodology precisely as their informational load peaks.

**Earliest observables, all cheap:** response-rate metadata published with the surveys; revision-magnitude tracking (a rolling average of absolute revisions is a data-quality index in one line); sub-index discontinuation announcements; the divergence between official series and their private shadows (payroll processors, price scrapes, transaction data). **Recommended as a standing annotation, not a scenario:** a data-confidence footnote in the Monthly, per pillar, downgrading language automatically when the underlying series' quality metrics deteriorate. **Rewires:** everything, which is the point — this is the only class that degrades all twenty-five tripwires simultaneously.

## M6 · The cloud–market-cap wrong-way loop **[partially in the literature; the loop is not]**

**Mechanism.** The official sector has the pieces: the FSB flags third-party and cloud concentration as a systemic vulnerability; a UK parliamentary committee wants major AI and cloud providers designated critical third parties by end-2026; BIS work documents concentration across every layer of the AI supply chain. What the literature does not assemble is the **wrong-way risk**: the same three or four companies are simultaneously (a) the largest weights in the index, (b) critical operational infrastructure for the exchanges, banks, and clearing systems that would have to process any crisis, and (c) the counterparties whose capex sustains the market's earnings narrative. A major cloud or dependency failure — the 2024 CrowdStrike outage grounding airlines and halting bank branches is the rehearsal, a *vendor* fault propagating through a hyperscaler distribution layer — is scenario 13 through the side door, **and it lands on scenario 20 at the same instant**, because the operational event and the index event share tickers. Wrong-way risk is the insurance reader's native concept: the exposure is largest exactly when the counterparty is impaired. The market has never priced an event that simultaneously disrupts settlement *and* craters the top of the index through the same names, and no stress test runs the two together.

**Earliest observables:** critical-third-party designations (a regulatory calendar item); cloud-concentration disclosures now trickling into bank filings; incident post-mortems for propagation breadth. **Rewires:** 13, 20, 3, and the settlement cluster's aliasing map.

## M7 · The insurance retreat — a slow-motion housing event **[the framework's home turf]**

**Mechanism.** Climate-driven insurer withdrawal from coastal and wildfire markets is usually filed under ESG commentary. Filed correctly, it is a *credit* chain with a decade fuse: carriers non-renew or exit; state FAIR plans balloon into concentrated, thinly capitalized insurers of last resort; premiums compound at double digits against flat incomes until **insurability, not affordability, becomes the binding constraint on the mortgage**. An uninsurable house is an unmortgageable house; unmortgageable houses reprice; repricing erodes property-tax bases; tax-base erosion stresses municipal credit; and the mortgage collateral sits on exactly the regional-bank balance sheets of scenario 14. Meanwhile the FAIR plans themselves carry assessment structures that transmit a bad season *back* onto the surviving private carriers — scenario 17's forced-seller mechanism with a statutory amplifier. The chain runs entirely outside macro data until its final two links.

**Earliest observables:** FAIR plan policy counts and concentration; non-renewal rates by state; homeowners premium growth against income; the first lender to formalize insurance-contingent underwriting; reinsurance renewal pricing for the exposed perils, which the January renewal series in scenario 17's build item already covers. **Rewires:** 14, 17, and the Monthly's housing pillar — and it is the class where this framework's professional edge over every macro shop is largest.

## M8 · Synthetic information attacks on market events **[known precedent, unpriced regime]**

**Mechanism.** A fabricated image of an explosion near the Pentagon moved US equity indexes for several minutes in May 2023, with essentially primitive tooling. The current regime is structurally more vulnerable, for a reason internal to this framework: **guidance has been withdrawn on principle**, so policy-path dispersion is wide and the market is calibrated to move violently on words — a September hike repriced from 35% to 57% on one legitimate speech. A synthetic Fed chairman announcing an intermeeting move, a fabricated issuer attestation (scenario 4's reserve trust runs on documents), or a spoofed casualty event at a chokepoint are all attacks on the *information layer* that every fast tripwire in Tier A trusts implicitly. Prediction markets — promoted this cycle in the media scan as macro infrastructure — double as a manipulation surface: thin books, headline authority, and now newsroom citation create a loop where a small position moves a "probability" that moves the tape. The IMF's 2026 cyber work adds the supply side: AI has collapsed the cost of producing the artifact.

**Earliest observable:** none reliable, and per the scenario 13 doctrine, no decorative proxy. The correct posture is resilience: a verification-latency protocol — pre-deciding which sources authenticate a market-moving claim and how many minutes the framework waits before its language moves. **Rewires:** 10, 16, 20, and every Tier A tripwire's trust model.

## M9 · The backstop ladder — moral hazard as accumulating structure **[the meta-mutation]**

**Mechanism.** Read the precedent sections of Part 1 in sequence and a pattern emerges that no individual scenario states: every crisis of the past two decades was resolved by *expanding the rescue perimeter* — money funds guaranteed in 2008, fallen angels bought in 2020, a term facility invented for bank duration in 2023, FIMA repo promoted for foreign holders in 2026. Each expansion was correct in the moment; each taught the market to pre-position for the next one; and several scenarios in this paper (4's depeg, 24's cascade, M3's retirement gate) derive their *contained* readings from an assumed official response. The mutation is twofold. First, **the ladder compounds**: each rescue enlarges the leverage that requires the next, and the perimeter now plausibly includes stablecoins, retirement-held private credit, and the Treasury basis trade. Second — the branch nobody prices — **the rung that gets refused**. A chairman whose entire public identity is discipline (thesis A of the Warsh question) refusing a rescue the market had pre-positioned for would convert a contained scenario into an uncontained one *by surprise*, which is precisely how the framework's own 2008 analog began. The terminal rung is Factor IV itself: the ladder ends where the backstopper's own balance sheet becomes the risk, at which point every "contained" reading in this paper is conditioned on capacity that no longer exists.

**Earliest observable:** the pricing of the put itself — skew and tail pricing collapsing *because* rescue is assumed; facility usage becoming routine (scenario 1's re-specification trigger is one instance of this class). **Rewires:** every contained reading in Families A, B, F and G. This class should be added to the Calibration Check as a standing question: *which of this cycle's benign readings assume a backstop, and what is the evidence the backstop is still there?*

## M10 · Time-structure mutations — the market loses its clock **[not in the literature]**

**Mechanism.** The entire risk architecture of modern markets quietly assumes a market that *closes*: circuit breakers reset overnight, margin is called against a settlement day, NAVs strike at 4pm, and the weekend is when authorities assemble rescues (every major intervention of the last two decades was announced on a Sunday night). Three current developments erode the assumption simultaneously: round-the-clock equity trading expanding through overnight venues and exchange filings; tokenized equities and funds trading continuously on chain rails while their hedges observe business hours; and zero-dated options concentrating an enormous share of index optionality into intraday gamma that expires and re-arms daily. The mutations follow directly: gap risk migrates to hours when hedging is impossible; a Sunday-night rescue has no closed market to land in; and the wrapper arbitrage of scenario 25 acquires windows where the ETF trades and the underlying cannot. The weekend — the system's maintenance window — is being deprecated without a replacement.

**Earliest observables:** overnight-session share of volume; tokenized-equity volume against primary-market hours; the first stress event that *starts* in an overnight session. **Rewires:** 20, 25, 2 (the carry unwind of August 2024 began in Asian hours), and the operational assumptions of every Tier A instrument, all of which currently sample on a business-day clock.

## Coda

The twenty-five are not predictions and they are not a portfolio. They are a set of mechanisms with instruments attached, maintained so that when something breaks, the framework recognizes the shape of it early enough to change its language rather than its story.

Four disciplines carry forward from this edition. **The second-order scan recurs**: Part 3 should be re-run annually or on any structural event, because a mutation map ages exactly as fast as the market structure it describes. **Conditioners, never triggers**, because the effective sample size forbids anything stronger. **Gaps stay visible**, because a decorative instrument is worse than an acknowledged absence, and scenario 13 is the standing proof. And **mutations get mapped before the world forces the rewrite**, because scenario 7's re-specification was correct and reactive, and the whole purpose of adding the Mutations element is to make the next one anticipatory.

The build queue in Part 2 is the operational output of this paper. The routing matrix is its reference layer. The re-specification log is how both stay honest as the world moves.

What remains unaddressed is the framing argument of §0.1, and it should stay unaddressed rather than be papered over: the event that ends up mattering most is not on this list, because it cannot be. Everything here widens the lit area. Nothing here illuminates the dark.
