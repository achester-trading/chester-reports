Title: The Operator. Shelf: Craft. Numeral XXXV. Purpose: the behavioral canon applied to one discretionary operator running a systematic reporting stack - what goes wrong between a correct read and a bad trade, and what the system can and cannot do about it. Written from principles now; the register's two months of data illustrate, labelled "early", and never conclude.
Parts. I The gap between the read and the trade: why a right view still loses money; the four failure modes the Doctrine already records (size creep, thesis attachment, overtrading in chop, narrative capture by one's own research), each defined, each with the tell that precedes it and the ledger field that would catch it. II The canon: the behavioral literature's findings that matter to a trader rather than an investor - loss aversion and the disposition effect, the house-money and break-even effects after a loss, overconfidence after a run, recency, anchoring on entry price, the narrative fallacy - stated as mechanisms with their sample and the conditions under which each has held or failed; then the Wizards' behavioral lessons (from the Market Wizards Alpha Framework already in the library: edge before signal, asymmetry before hit rate, size to opportunity, do-nothing is a position) restated as counters to those mechanisms. III The system's answer: what the Doctrine, the register, the DTH-1 rules (the day-trading order filed 6 Nov 2026 as Addendum A to Amendment #5: a resting stop, a one-line thesis, 1% and 2% limits, no flip inside 30 minutes, the view line) and the scorecards do - rules that constrain execution without constraining direction - and what they cannot do; a short chapter on the view line as the one instrument that makes a gut call scoreable. IV Routed items: the signal-triage items the register routes to this paper (the dip-buying cohort and the rally trap from the earlier rounds; the viral dated-call type of item 132) read as behavioral objects. V Tactical Applications by horizon (what the day trader, the swing trader and the allocator each check before and after), Wiring (the plan, the close's rule-by-rule check, the Weekly scorecard, the Doctrine sitting), Environments (which failure modes each regime invites: chop, trend, negative gamma, event weeks). Appendix A, Assumptions and Falsifiers: what would show this paper wrong (a compliant book that underperforms a non-compliant one over 100 trades, say). Appendix B, dated: the register's early figures, labelled early; the week of 26 Oct 2026 as a sketch only - the full worked case is Draft 2's, from register data, and no dollar or account figure appears anywhere (standing rule).
Boundaries. Portfolio Construction owns sizing across books; the Operating Doctrine owns the rules; this paper owns the operator. Cite both by name.
