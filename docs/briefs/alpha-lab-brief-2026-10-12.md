## 1. What "unrestrained" should mean, and the one thing it cannot

Unrestrained in **search**: any instrument the data covers, any horizon from minutes to months, long or short, outright or options or futures or crypto, any method — rules, statistics, machine learning — no narrative required, no white paper to cite, no sitting to approve a trade. The layer decides and executes on its own schedule, in paper, and reports what it did. The Doctrine's books, bands, dials and fences do not reach into it. The reports do not drive it; it reads the same store they read.

Unrestrained in **risk** is not profitability; it is variance. A system that maximises expected value with no drawdown constraint has an expected value that goes to the sky and a probability of ruin that goes to one — the gambler's-ruin result, not an opinion. The objective that actually means "singular focus on profitability" is the one Kelly wrote down: **maximise the expected growth rate of capital, net of costs, subject to a maximum-drawdown constraint**, sized at a fraction of full Kelly because the edge is estimated, not known. Every professional systematic book is built on that objective; the ones that weren't are not around. So the layer has exactly four restraints, and three of them are already yours: the blocklist (law), paper until the 2027 gate (your rule — and paper means ledger entries against stored prices, no order path, as you ruled on 2 Oct), a risk governor (the objective itself), and an evidence gate — no engine trades paper capital until it has passed validation (§4). Nothing else.

## 2. What "world class" means at this scale, honestly

The large systematic shops' edge is data, execution and discipline more than ideas. At one operator's scale, the achievable world-class property is **process**: point-in-time integrity (no look-ahead, ever), realistic costs, honesty about multiple testing, Kelly-fractional sizing, and a kill criterion every engine is born with. Most retail systems fail on exactly those, which is why a system that gets them right is already ahead. Three things work in your favour and the funds' disfavour: the FlashAlpha dealer-positioning data (GEX, DEX, vanna, charm, the surface) is institutional-grade and few individuals have it; the store already holds prediction-market, positioning, funding and options series point-in-time; and small size means capacity is no constraint — you can trade things a fund cannot (thin options, small crypto venues, prediction-market mispricings) without moving them.

The thing that works against you is the one the Doctrine was written for: the operator's own history says discretion has been the expensive part. The Alpha Lab's answer is that there is no discretion inside it — the operator sets the objective, the restraints and the roster, and then reads the ledger.

## 3. Architecture — independent by construction

- **Its own tree, ledger and book.** `alpha/` beside the existing packages; its own SQLite ledger (`alpha.db`: engines, signals, simulated fills, marks, grades, and the variant count behind every validation); its own paper book with its own notional capital, never netted with the Doctrine books' paper. It reads the observation store through the same point-in-time API the reports use (`as_of` = the store's `available_at`), **read-only**: it never writes to the store, the register, the narrative ledger or any report. The reports may print one line from its ledger — "Alpha Lab: +x.x% since <date>, n engines live, drawdown y%" — for transparency; nothing flows the other way beyond the three bridges of §7.
- **Engines.** An engine is a module with a fixed interface: `universe()`, `signal(as_of)`, `size(signal, risk_budget)`, `exits()`, a declared horizon, a declared cost model, a kill criterion, an `engine_id`. Engines are independent of each other; the allocator combines them.
- **The allocator.** Fractional Kelly (quarter-Kelly to start) across engines on their estimated edge and covariance from the ledger; a portfolio heat cap; correlated engines counted once. Rebalanced weekly from the ledger, never by hand.
- **The risk governor.** Engine-level: at −10% from its own high-water mark the engine halves; at −20% it stops and goes back to validation. Portfolio-level: at −15% the whole layer stops for review. These numbers are decisions (§9), and they are the only hand on the wheel.
- **Execution — ledger-paper, not orders.** Each engine's trades are ledger entries filled against stored prices on its own schedule — a daily engine at the close, a weekly engine on Sunday, an intraday engine on its bar clock once bars are stored — at the stored bid or ask where a quote exists (the 6b chain history for options, the contract record W-1.1 adds for prediction markets, venue prices for crypto) and at the next bar's open plus the cost model where only bars exist; exits are evaluated against the stored bars every session, bracket-style. No order of any kind is sent anywhere: that is the 2 Oct ruling and this layer keeps it. The gate ladder is the only route out of ledger-paper — broker-paper through the order gate after Gate 1.5, live after Gate 3 — and both rungs are yours to rule, not the lab's.
- **The cost model is the honesty.** Ledger-paper flatters any system that ignores costs, so every engine's fill carries a modelled spread, slippage by size, commission, borrow, roll or funding, and the model is calibrated conservatively — half the quoted spread crossed, never the mid — until Gate 1.5's real paper fills can calibrate it.
- **The clock.** Runs on the box as its own systemd unit on its own timers; never inside a report's run; failures are its own and never stop a report.

## 4. The validation standard — the evidence gate

No engine trades paper capital until it has passed all of these, and the pass is recorded:

1. **Point-in-time only.** Every input read at its `available_at`; a backtest that touches a revised vintage is void.
2. **Walk-forward with an embargo.** Parameters fitted on a window, tested on the next, rolled; an embargo gap equal to the engine's horizon between fit and test; the fit windows never see the test windows' outcomes.
3. **A cost model per instrument**: spread, slippage by size, commissions, borrow for shorts, roll for futures, funding for perps, FX for the baht and foreign names. An engine's edge is stated after costs or not at all.
4. **A multiple-testing haircut.** The number of variants tried is counted and the reported Sharpe is deflated for it (the deflated Sharpe ratio); an engine whose deflated Sharpe is below one at its sample size does not pass, whatever the raw figure says.
5. **A minimum sample** at the engine's horizon — at least 100 independent signals for a daily engine, 40 for a weekly one, 400 for an intraday one — before any size above the minimum.
6. **An out-of-sample paper period** — the first N signals trade at minimum size regardless of backtest; the allocator may raise the size only on the paper ledger's own evidence.
7. **A kill criterion fixed at birth**: retired when, after the minimum sample, its expectancy after modelled costs is at or below zero, or its realised Sharpe is below half the deflated Sharpe it was validated with; never resized up before the sample. (The Enterprise Layer's own engines keep their rule — forty graded trades, expectancy or Brier against the base rate; this is the lab's, chosen to catch an edge that was real in the backtest and is not in paper.)

## 5. The engine roster — what the data can support, in the order the evidence suggests

Each is a hypothesis to test under §4, not a promise. Ranked by documented edge and by how much of the data you already hold.

1. **Volatility risk premium, defined-risk, dealer-conditioned.** Short SPY/QQQ volatility through defined-risk structures (iron condors, put spreads, call spreads), conditioned on the dealer regime from FlashAlpha — positive-gamma regimes, vanna and charm flows into expiry, the implied-against-realised gap the Weekly already prints (14.8 against 10 last week). The VRP is the best-documented premium in liquid markets; the dealer conditioning is your differentiated data. Daily and weekly expiries; the 0DTE variant waits for intraday data.
2. **Cross-asset trend and momentum.** Time-series and cross-sectional momentum across the ~90 stored instruments and the crypto set, at one-to-twelve-week horizons, long and short, volatility-targeted. The most replicated anomaly in the literature; works with discipline and dies with discretion, which is why it belongs in a layer with none.
3. **Prediction-market against futures disagreement.** When Kalshi or Polymarket and fed-funds futures price the same meeting more than a threshold apart (the Weekly flagged 54% against 71% on the March hike), take the cheaper side in paper on the venue with the better executable price, hold to resolution or convergence. Close to an arbitrage, capacity-limited, and no fund bothers — exactly a small operator's edge. Needs the contract-level record W-1.1 added (bid, ask, liquidity).
4. **Scheduled-event engines.** CPI, payrolls, FOMC: the surprise against consensus, conditioned on positioning (the §10A rows) and the dealer regime, traded in the first session's drift or the vol crush after; earnings in the 29-name universe: the post-earnings-announcement drift and the implied-move-against-realised trade, conditioned on the chain store. Base rates first (the system already grades these), engine second.
5. **Crypto carry and basis.** Perp funding harvest and spot–perp basis on BTC and ETH across Hyperliquid, Bybit and OKX (the S-3 funding and open-interest series the architecture guide already specifies), delta-neutral, with the funding regime as the switch. Documented, capacity-irrelevant at your size, and your data order already captures it.
6. **Mean reversion at extremes.** The move screen's 2.5σ prints with their false-flag base rates: fade the ones whose own history says they reverse, at the horizon the history says. The screen was built today; the base rates accrue from its first run.
7. **Intraday dealer-flow regimes (0DTE).** After 6b's intraday captures: opening-range and VWAP behaviour conditioned on the gamma regime and the 0DTE flow — the Dealer's Hand Part V as an engine rather than a report.

Not on the roster, deliberately: anything discretionary; anything on a Brookfield-complex name; anything that needs leverage the paper account cannot model honestly; single-name stock picking from fundamentals (the Doctrine's value warning holds for a machine too — research hours are the scarce input and the edge is small).

## 7. How it relates to everything else

The reports stay the operator's intelligence; the Doctrine's books stay the operator's capital. The lab is the machine's book, in ledger-paper, running beside them and graded on the same ledger standard. Three bridges only: the reports print its one-line ledger; its graded engines are tabled at the Doctrine sittings as evidence — an engine that proves out is a candidate for the gate ladder (broker-paper after Gate 1.5, live after Gate 3, not before 2027), by your ruling each time, never by the lab's; and the lab's cost models, walk-forward harness and deflated-Sharpe gate are shared infrastructure the Enterprise Layer's two engines may use when they register, under the Doctrine's own rules. Nothing in the lab changes a report, a dial, a band or a packet, and the Enterprise Layer's intraday plan (the state vector, the two engines, the tactics layer) is unchanged — the lab is a third thing beside them, not a replacement.

## 8. The learning loop — how the lab gets better without fooling itself

"Continuously learning" is where most trading systems die, not where they live: a system that re-fits itself to the last month is a system that is always perfectly tuned to the regime that just ended. So the loop is built on one principle — **the lab may learn anything inside its constitution and nothing about its constitution** — and on four loops at four cadences, each of which writes a versioned, reversible, counterfactually graded event to the ledger.

**The constitution (not learnable; changed only by your ruling):** the four restraints; the governor's numbers; the validation standard of §4; the kill criterion; the hold-out rule below; the variant count. Everything else is the loop's.

1. **Nightly — learn from the ledger.** After the close: mark every open position, grade every closed one, and update each engine's edge estimate as a posterior — win rate and expectancy with a shrinkage prior toward zero, so a lucky fortnight is not sized up and a sample of twelve is not believed. Recalibrate the cost model from the ledger's modelled fills against the stored quotes (and, after Gate 1.5, against real paper fills). Recompute the allocator's weights from the posteriors and the engines' realised covariance. Attribute the day's P&L by regime — the dials' states, the volatility regime, the dealer regime, event days — so the loop knows *where* each engine earns and where it loses. Check each engine's drift: its rolling out-of-sample metric against the one it was validated with, flagged below the lower band. This loop changes sizes and weights; it never changes an engine's rules.

2. **Weekly — re-validate, re-fit within bounds, and write the research agenda.** Sunday, after the Weekly's run: extend every live engine's walk-forward by the week; re-estimate its parameters **within the ranges they were validated with** — a parameter moves only if the deflated out-of-sample metric improves on the embargoed window, every re-fit is counted as one more variant in the deflation, and a parameter that wants to leave its range is a new engine, through the gate. Then write `alpha/research/agenda.md` from the residuals: where the lab lost, by regime, instrument and day; what moved that it did not trade (the move screen's prints without a position); how correlated the engines have become (crowding on one bet); and the backtest-to-paper gap per engine. The agenda is what the innovation loop reads.

3. **Fortnightly — innovate: generate, pre-register, test, promote.** Two sources of hypotheses, one gate. *Systematic:* a grammar of engine components — signal families × conditioning variables (the dials, the dealer regime, positioning, the funding regime) × horizons × instruments × exits — searched with the deflated Sharpe counting every candidate tried, so the search is honest about its own size. *Model-proposed:* a research sitting — a scheduled session paste, "the Lab research sitting" — in which a session reads the agenda and the system's own material and proposes at most five hypotheses, each **pre-registered before any backtest runs**: the mechanism in a paragraph, the expected sign and size, the sample it needs, its kill criterion, written to `alpha/hypotheses/` first. Pre-registration is what separates innovation from data-mining; a hypothesis written after the backtest is a variant, not an idea. The system's own material is the richest source: every Weekly tell, every Doctrine tell, every scanner setup and every graded forecast source in the probability ledger is a testable statement — "does this predict at horizon *h* after costs?" — and the loop tests each one as a candidate engine, which is how the reports' intelligence reaches the lab without the reports driving it. Every hypothesis then goes through §4 unchanged. **The ones that pass enter paper at minimum size on the loop's own authority**; you are told in the Weekly's lab line and in a monthly Lab report, and you may retire any engine by ruling. The ones that fail are kept with their result, so the same idea is not re-tried under a new name — the register of failures is as much of the lab's knowledge as the roster.

4. **Quarterly — grade the learning itself.** Every change the loop made — a re-fit, a re-weight, a promotion, a retirement — is a versioned event with a counterfactual: what the lab would have earned without it, from the same stored prices. The loop's net contribution over the quarter is a line in the Quarterly's lab page; if its changes were net negative, the loop's own aggressiveness is reduced for the next quarter — re-fit bounds narrowed, re-fit cadence slowed — so the loop learns about itself. Two standing rules sit here: **no re-fitting inside a drawdown** (an engine the governor has halved is frozen, not re-optimised — re-optimising on the drawdown is the classic way to die), and a **permanent hold-out** — every fourth calendar month of history is never used for fitting by any loop, only for the final check before a promotion, so there is always data the lab has never seen.

What the loop may do alone: change sizes and allocator weights; recalibrate costs; re-fit parameters within validated ranges; promote a gate-passing engine into paper at minimum size; retire an engine under the kill rule; narrow its own bounds. What it may never do: touch the constitution; send an order; write to anything outside `alpha/`; raise an engine above minimum size before its sample; re-fit a halved engine; fit on the hold-out.
