I want you to critically assess, refine, and then design/implement a dedicated **Day Trading Layer (DTH-1)** within my broader trading architecture.

This layer must be operationally independent enough to exploit intraday opportunities, but it must also remain connected to the broader market framework, enterprise risk system, evidence ledger, research system, and multi-horizon market views.

Do **not** assume this specification is correct simply because I am providing it. Before implementation, critically challenge the design, simplify it where possible, identify duplication or missing controls, and recommend changes where warranted.

# I. PURPOSE

The Day Trading Layer exists to identify and exploit short-horizon opportunities arising from:

- intraday trend and momentum
- opening-range behavior
- overnight gaps
- buy-the-dip behavior
- sell-the-rip behavior
- sideways/choppy markets
- mean reversion
- breakouts and failed breakouts
- economic releases
- earnings and corporate events
- macro/news catalysts
- cross-asset dislocations
- relative-value opportunities
- options/dealer positioning
- volatility dislocations
- liquidity and order-flow anomalies
- temporary market inefficiencies
- idiosyncratic security-level events

The goal is **not maximum trading activity**.

The system must be comfortable concluding:

**NO TRADE — insufficient edge.**

The objective is risk-adjusted, empirically supported opportunity capture.

# II. RELATIONSHIP TO THE BROADER TRADING SYSTEM

The Day Trading Layer is a **domain-specific trading subsystem**, not the master intelligence or governance system.

It receives broader context from the Enterprise Trading Architecture but retains intraday decision independence.

The broader system should provide a standardized Strategic Directional View such as:

- BULLISH / TRENDING UP
- BEARISH / TRENDING DOWN
- SIDEWAYS / CHOP
- TRANSITION / UNCERTAIN

This strategic view may be derived from weekly, monthly, tactical, macro, regime, positioning, cross-asset, fundamental, and other enterprise-level analysis.

Treat this strategic directional view as a **prior, not a command**.

Examples:

If the broader environment is bullish:
- preferentially search for high-quality buy-the-dip opportunities;
- favor long setups where evidence is otherwise comparable;
- recognize that countertrend shorts may require stronger evidence or shorter holding periods.

If the broader environment is bearish:
- preferentially search for sell-the-rip opportunities;
- favor short setups where evidence is otherwise comparable.

If the broader environment is sideways/choppy:
- emphasize mean reversion, failed breakouts, range behavior, relative value, dispersion, volatility and tactical reversals.

If the broader environment is transitional or uncertain:
- reduce reliance on directional priors and require stronger intraday confirmation.

However, the Day Trading Layer must remain free to take a tactical trade contrary to the strategic direction when the evidence is sufficiently strong.

A bearish intraday trade inside a bullish monthly regime is not inherently contradictory.

The system must explicitly maintain **horizon-separated beliefs**.

For example:

- structural: bullish
- 1–3 months: bullish
- 1–4 weeks: sideways/bullish
- 1–5 days: neutral
- intraday: bearish tactical setup
- immediate trigger: sell-the-rip

Do not collapse these states into a single BUY / SELL / NEUTRAL opinion.

# III. BIDIRECTIONAL INFORMATION FLOW

Information must flow both ways.

### Enterprise → Day Trading

The Day Trading Layer should receive standardized enterprise context including:

- multi-horizon directional state
- market regime
- macro/event calendar
- systemic risk conditions
- relevant positioning
- broader volatility environment
- important structural or white-paper insights
- portfolio exposures and constraints
- known signal failure modes
- active enterprise-level cautions

### Day Trading → Enterprise

The Day Trading Layer should publish evidence back to the enterprise system, including persistent observations such as:

- repeated failed breakouts
- unusual breadth deterioration
- changing reaction to positive or negative news
- unusual auction behavior
- systematic buy-the-dip or sell-the-rip behavior
- increasing realized volatility
- liquidity deterioration
- unusual dealer-positioning effects
- new cross-asset relationships
- recurring anomalies
- new candidate signals
- evidence that a broader regime may be changing

A single intraday observation should not rewrite enterprise doctrine.

Persistent, statistically meaningful observations should become research hypotheses for enterprise evaluation.

# VII. INDEPENDENT ALPHA / OPPORTUNITY ENGINES

Maintain independent edge families rather than a single monolithic trading opinion.

Candidate engines include:

### 1. Overnight Gap / News
Evaluate gap continuation, gap fade, overnight inventory and material overnight catalysts.

### 2. Opening Range / Momentum
Study opening-range breaks, continuation, failed breaks and early institutional flow.

### 3. VWAP / Mean Reversion
Identify stretched price behavior and statistically justified reversion opportunities.

### 4. Event / Catalyst
Economic releases, earnings, guidance, regulatory developments, company events and unexpected news.

### 5. Options / Dealer Positioning
Use options positioning, volatility structure and dealer effects where empirically justified.

### 6. Relative Value / Dispersion
Pairs, sector/security divergence, ETF-component divergence, index dispersion and cross-asset inconsistency.

### 7. Volume / Flow / Microstructure
Volume anomalies, liquidity, breadth, auction behavior and other actionable flow information.

### 8. Volatility Dislocation
Differences among realized volatility, implied volatility, event risk and cross-instrument volatility pricing.

### 9. Market Anomaly
Unexpected relationships, price reactions, unusual correlations or behavior that does not fit established models.

### 10. Strategic-Regime Alignment
Measure whether an intraday setup is aligned with or fighting the broader directional/regime state.

Do not assume all ten require separate autonomous LLM agents.

Critically determine which should be:

- deterministic models
- statistical engines
- event-driven code
- specialized LLM analysis
- combined functions

Maintain independence among edge families where possible so apparent signal convergence is not simply multiple transformations of the same underlying information.

# XIII. LOCAL ADVERSARIAL REVIEW

Before a material trade reaches the Risk Governor, challenge it.

Ask:

- What would make this thesis wrong?
- What evidence contradicts it?
- Are supporting signals truly independent?
- Are we double-counting the same underlying information?
- Is this a crowded setup?
- Is the market reaction inconsistent with the narrative?
- Could the apparent edge be data-mined?
- Is a simpler causal explanation available?
- Is this setup historically regime-dependent?
- Are we reacting late?
- Is the expected edge large enough after costs?
- Is there asymmetric event risk?

Use tiered adversarial review.

Routine, small trades should receive fast automated checks.

Unusual, large, highly levered, low-liquidity, high-conviction or event-sensitive trades should trigger deeper review.
