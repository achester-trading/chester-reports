## 1. Why

The Alternative Asset report's depth was scattered by the redesign: the frames live in the papers (V Currencies, VI Credit, VII Energy, VIII Metals, IX Digital Assets, XXI International Equities, with IV Rates, I Foundations, II Tails, XVII Portfolio Construction and XX Base Rates behind them), the data is in the store or ruled into it, and since T3.1 each family has a paragraph in the Monthly's slow layers. Three things are missing. **One place where the families are read in depth on a cadence** — and the papers already say what that cadence should do: five of them end with a Cadence Summary whose quarterly line is the same sentence — *rewrite the scenario probabilities, review the falsification tests explicitly, and state which scenario the quarter's evidence supported* (VIII, VII, V, IX, VI). **A correlation layer nobody owns** — the former report's primary output, routed to by six tail scenarios, specified in the `real-assets` pillar, and built nowhere. **A thesis per family the system holds itself to** — the papers' scenario sets, ledgered and graded, rather than prose that is never revisited.

The operator wants all three, for intermediate- and long-horizon trading in these categories (Books A and B).

## 2. The operator's rulings of 10 Oct

1. A quarterly deep dive on five categories — **Metals (gold, silver, copper); Oil and its impact on the global economy; Currency; Emerging Markets, deeper on China including its local political environment; Crypto** with coin deep dives, emerging trends and more scanning — and lighter chapters on real estate, credit and international equities.
2. Correlations studied on an ongoing basis, and how they shift.
3. Read in 75–90 minutes; a longer reference is acceptable if well organised and high quality (ruled 10 Oct as one document with two paths, §3; decision 1).
4. Bar charts of every asset across 20-year, 5-year and 1-year horizons.
5. A deep qualitative supply/demand assessment per category and a news scan for emerging stories and issues.

8. Coin list BTC, ETH, SOL, ZEC, PRL, with a lighter tier to be proposed.
9. *(this revision)* The reporting builds from the white papers and probes the dynamics they suggest; institutional practice is considered.

## 3. The document: one edition, two paths

- **The read (≤ 90 minutes):** the executive summary (one paragraph per chapter, built last from the finished chapters, audited, as the Monthly's), then each chapter's **lead page** — last quarter's theses graded, the new theses, the quarter in five lines, the three charts that carry it, the one-line verdict on each of the paper's falsification tests. Reading time printed for the read.
- **The reference (no budget):** the full chapter beneath its lead — structure, supply and demand, the analyses of §5, correlations, the scan's stories, the series tables — organised identically in every chapter (§4.1), so the reader always knows where a thing is.
- Rendered by the stack renderer at a fourth cadence, `quarterly`, with its own depth column in `config/reporting_stack.yaml` (the T2.6 design: one stack, four cadences); the lead/reference split is a cadence feature. The Reader's Guide (XXXIII §4.8) lists the Quarterly Structural as "specified, not built, no specification document yet"; this order builds the cadence the Structural will also use.
- **How the chapters sit in the stack.** The stack's ten sections are the Quarterly's **front matter** at quarterly depth — the read (the executive summary and the quarter's slate), the tape (the horizon bars, the quilt, the cross-asset volatility table), what doesn't fit, plumbing and rates (the global regime: growth, inflation, policy, fiscal, the dollar, liquidity, from the dials and dimensions), positioning and flows (cross-asset, by the standard in §10A), what's priced, narratives (voices), ahead (the calendar and the scenario table), the book (the draft slate of §6) — and the family chapters are the Quarterly's **slow layers**, as the alternative-asset paragraphs are the Monthly's. One fixed figure opens the front matter: the **transmission map**, six arrows drawn from the papers (energy → CPI → policy → discount rate; dollar and funding → EM flows, external debt, commodities, crypto leverage; China demand and property → industrial metals, Asian exporters, EM credit; liquidity shock → deleveraging → correlation convergence; fiscal and sovereign risk → term premium, FX risk premium, real assets; stablecoin disruption → bill demand, collateral, venues) — hypotheses to be read against the quarter, never causal laws.
- **Cadence:** the second weekend after quarter-end (the Saturday). First edition **Sat 9 Jan 2027 for 4Q26**; dry run in December. The quarterly scan (§8) fires the Monday of the edition's week.

## 4. The chapters

### 4.1 The chapter template (every category, deep or light)

1. **Last quarter's theses, graded** — hit, miss or not yet due, with the Brier and one sentence on why (§6).
2. **Theses** — the 3-month and 12-month theses: the paper's scenario set rewritten with this quarter's probabilities, each with its measurable, its falsifier and the Book it informs.
3. **The quarter** — what moved, in the stack's words, with the three horizon bars (§7) and the family's long-frame chart; then **why**: the driver attribution row (§5 — the family's quarterly return against real rates, the broad dollar, the growth and inflation surprises and the family's own supply proxy, with the explained share and the residual), so a thesis can be graded as right for the right reason.
3a. **What's priced** — the curve (spot against the 12-month forward and the roll), the options-implied range where the chain is logged (GLD, SLV, USO, IBIT, FXE, FXY), and the agency or consensus forecast against spot with its revision since last quarter (§8); the gap between what is priced and what the structure says is the chapter's standing question.
4. **Structure** — the paper's Tier 1 and Tier 2 watches at quarterly resolution: positioning, flows, inventories, reserves, issuance, each with dual percentiles (five-year and full history), by the positioning standard of §10A (disaggregated COT categories for commodities, TFF for financial futures, positions as a share of open interest; ETF flows from share counts, never from AUM; flow, position and price-driven AUM kept apart).
5. **Supply and demand** — the paper's Tier 3 fundamentals (the agency balances, §8) plus the qualitative assessment, which answers the same four questions in every family, because the papers converge on them: *who is the marginal buyer and the marginal seller this quarter* (VIII §1, IX §20); *what is the buffer or the effective float* (VII §1, the `real-assets` elasticity); *what binds — the constraint that sets price* (VII, VIII §15); *what would make the marginal buyer step away.* Every claim with its source line (§8).
6. **The analyses** — the named analyses of §5 for this family, each printed with its state and its kill condition.
7. **Falsification tests** — the paper's own tests, one line each: holds / strained / failed this quarter, with the evidence.
8. **Correlations** — the family's named rows and pairs from the correlation layer (§9): current, one-year, five-year distribution, shifts this quarter, the peer-pair divergences.
9. **Emerging stories, and what changed in the world** — the scan's items for the family with sources; what the system does not yet measure (the paper's Gaps, standing); and one required line even when the answer is "nothing": *structural change this quarter — none confirmed / developing: … / confirmed: …*, with the status drawn from the tail watch's re-specification log and the signal-intake register, never declared freehand.
10. **The book** — what the chapter implies for Book A (allocation across regimes) and Book B (weeks to months), **as draft register entries**: each deep chapter yields at least one draft per horizon — a Book B draft for the 3-month thesis and a Book A draft for the 12-month — or an explicit *no draft* line naming the reason (no edge, liquidity, data quality); each draft carries the register's fields (instrument, direction, thesis, edge, horizon, size, invalidation, counter-thesis), its portfolio role in XVII's words (structural / proxy / direct / convex, or carry / growth / tactical), and the instrument's cost row (spread, roll, borrow, FX hedge from the IBKR costs writer); drafts are graded by the register's grader from the day they are written, taken or not. Never recommendations; the blocklist at write time; in the paper's own instrument and sizing terms (VIII §24–26, VII §27–29, V §30–33, IX §26).
11. **The ten questions** (reference only) — the paper's Appendix D "Ten Questions", answered for the quarter in one line each (VIII, VII, V, IX, VI carry one).

The lead page also carries a **confidence line**: the weakest-link disclosure for the chapter's series, the Brier of the chapter's theses to date, and the standing DISSENT from the voices register.

Light chapters (real estate, credit, international equities, agriculture) use items 1–4, 7, 8, 10; Thailand uses the full template less item 11, under its isolation rule.

### 4.2 Metals — gold, silver, copper (paper VIII)

**Frame.** Price is where the marginal buyer and seller agree; stock-versus-flow is "the single most important concept" (gold ~60:1, so price is holder behaviour; copper weeks of consumption, so deficits ration by price; silver migrating toward flow-dominated after six deficits); gold's real-yield model broke in 2022 when a price-insensitive reserve manager replaced the Western buyer; "physical market signals lead financial market signals".

**Structure (T1/T2 quarterly).** Official-sector purchases (WGC GDT, with the estimate for unreported buying; the 200t+/quarter bull line, two quarters of net selling the bear line); gold ETF holdings (GLD/IAU tonnage); 10-year real yield (DFII10) and the trade-weighted dollar beside gold; COMEX managed-money percentiles for gold, silver and copper (the 85th/15th crowded/washed-out rule; SR-42 extends copper COT, this order extends gold and silver); copper TC/RCs; LME + COMEX + SHFE inventories read together (concurrent decline = signal, divergence = relocation; the Section 232 COMEX–LME spread as the two-tier read); silver COMEX + LBMA inventories; lease rates and EFP where free; SGE and Yangshan premiums (the China physical read); the gold/silver ratio with its reversal zones (>90 / <45; rebalance >~85 / <~50); Chilean output (Cochilco).

**Supply and demand (T3).** The WGC GDT balance by category (jewellery, technology, investment bar-and-coin and ETF, central banks; mine, recycling) — the one table institutional gold decks always carry; the Silver Institute World Silver Survey (April) and Interim (autumn); the ICSG monthly bulletin; AISC and the WGC cost curve (the soft floor); solar silver demand against ex-solar industrial demand; copper's grid, EV and data-centre build; Chinese property, PMI and grid investment.

**The analyses.** The gold residual and the two-bid decomposition; effective-float elasticity; the sovereign gold-share convergence gap; gold–equity correlation by shock type; copper/gold against the 10-year (§5).

**Falsification tests (seven, VIII §4).** The official bid (floor falsified below ~500t annualised while price falls); the real-yield link dormant not dead (falsified if gold resumes a tight inverse correlation to TIPS); target-driven buying stops when targets are met; silver's deficit binding (falsified by COMEX/LBMA rebuilding or two more years without constraint — lease rates must rise); solar's −30% offset by ex-solar demand; TC/RC recovery as the early warning; aluminium substitution and scrap response.

**Scenarios to rewrite.** Per metal bull / base / bear ranges (gold $5,500–7,000 / $4,200–5,500 / $3,200–4,200; silver $85–120 / $55–75 / $35–55; copper $14,000–17,000 / $11,000–13,500 / $8,500–11,000 per tonne, as the paper dates them) and the ten-year families (gold deepening ~30 / persisting ~40 / exhausting ~30; silver transition ~35 / industrial disappoints ~40 / never binds ~25; copper structural deficit ~40 / substitution ~35 / demand disappointment ~25).

**Deck items added.** Miners versus the metal (GDX/GLD, SIL/SLV) — the paper notes the lag and says it "often resolves"; the quarter's premium/discount map (SGE, India, retail coin premiums, PSLV NAV).

### 4.3 Oil, and its impact on the global economy (paper VII; IV for transmission)

**Frame.** "Energy is a spare-capacity and inventory market, not a resource market": the buffer sets the price, and shock sensitivity is inversely proportional to it; "the front month lies — the curve is the most informative single object"; electricity has become the scarce energy commodity. The chapter reads oil through its buffer and then follows the transmission the Foundations paper calls the master coupling of 2026 — energy → CPI → policy → discount rate.

**Structure (T1/T2 quarterly).** The buffer table (VII App. C) refreshed: crude versus the five-year band with the SPR beside it; Brent M1–M6 and M1–M12 (the spot–12-month gap as the disruption premium, its decay as confidence in resolution); Gulf exports and reachability-adjusted spare capacity; US gas storage against the band, LNG feedgas, TTF and EU storage; PJM's auction against the cap and the shortfall; uranium term, SWU and waivers. Supply response: rigs, frac spreads, DUCs; OPEC+ output versus quota; refinery utilisation and cracks; COT CL/NG percentiles; OVX and crude skew (call skew = disruption hedging, put skew = glut fear).

**Supply and demand (T3).** The three agency balances side by side — EIA STEO, IEA OMR, OPEC MOMR (demand, supply, stock change, OECD days of cover) — with the quarter's revisions logged (the paper's "store forecast levels with dates so revisions are auto-detected"); OPEC+ decisions as events; the restoration decision after any reopening ("rapid = the 1986 setup, $70 in play; managed = $85–95 holds").

**The transmission set (the "economy" half).** Commodity pass-through — commodity YoY against CPI YoY with its 3–6-month lead and the kill rule (§5); energy's weight in CPI and the gasoline retail series; oil in gold terms; oil versus breakevens ("a rally breakevens ignore is being read as temporary"); the oil–dollar sign (rising together = supply shock with a haven bid, maximum pain for energy-importing EM — the EM chapter's cross-reference); Dallas Fed producer breakevens; Gulf fiscal breakevens against Brent (tail S21: SWF selling loads when oil falls below the weighted breakeven for two quarters); the petrodollar leg in TIC where visible.

**The analyses.** The disruption–price divergence (PortWatch transits ÷ 85/day against Brent) with the two-quarter rule, pre-dated; the pass-through residual (§5).

**Falsification tests (seven, VII §4).** Spare capacity behind Hormuz is not spare capacity; the buffer sets price (a 5 mb/d shock producing a sub-$15 move at the five-year low kills it); HH–TTF convergence (<$5 with exports at capacity falsifies the bottleneck); the LNG glut deferred not repealed (TTF >$15 after the Qatar and US trains); the power squeeze structural (two PJM auctions below cap with the shortfall closed); data-centre load real (withdrawals > energisations four quarters, or capex cut 30%+); enrichment the constraint (SWU falling while term U3O8 rises).

**Scenarios to rewrite.** The twelve-month four — managed normalisation ~40, prolonged disruption ~30, glut returns ~15, AI-capex bust ~15 — with Brent, HH/TTF and PJM markers; the ten-year oil, gas and power families.

**Deck items added.** The balance table above; the roll-yield row for every futures-held commodity (the paper's "USO/UNG roll cost 10–30%/yr in contango" is the reason it belongs on the page).

**The second half: gas, power and uranium.** The paper's other three buffers get their own sub-chapter rather than a row in oil's table, because their constraints are different in kind — the seasonal storage band and the LNG linkage for gas (Henry Hub, TTF, JKM kept separate: "benchmarks are not fungible without liquefaction, shipping and regasification"); the reserve margin, the interconnection queue and the turbine backlog for power, with the PJM auction as "the most important scheduled datapoint"; utility inventory and Western enrichment for uranium. Oil substitution is read with the paper's discipline — operating capacity and utilisation, not announced projects; EVs displace road fuel, nuclear and renewables displace oil only where oil-fired power exists — from the IEA's EV and renewables outlooks through the scan.

### 4.4 Currency (paper V; IV Tier 6; GTM-22)

**Frame.** A currency price is always a pair — "devaluation needs a denominator"; four channels (rate differentials, growth and capital flows, trade, risk/liquidity/reserve status — the dollar smile), each with its horizon; three devaluations (Dev-1 cyclical, Dev-2 debasement against real assets, Dev-3 loss of reserve status); gold is "the anti-currency" and in 2025 passed Treasuries in official reserves — "the most important datapoint". The Rates paper homes its Tier 6 signals here: S29 the dollar, S30 JGB yields, S31 gold plus central-bank demand.

**The pair universe, named.** The chapter names its pairs instead of treating FX as one market. *Deep basket* (a page each): EUR/USD, USD/JPY, GBP/USD, USD/CHF, AUD/USD, USD/CAD, USD/CNY and USD/CNH kept separate, USD/THB. *Scan tier* (one table, from FRED's daily H.10 set, free): NZD, SEK, NOK, MXN, BRL, ZAR, KRW, TWD, INR, SGD, HKD and the crosses that isolate a thesis (EUR/JPY, AUD/JPY, EUR/CHF, EUR/GBP). Pair priority is refreshed each quarter on depth, hedgeability, capital controls and the operator's exposure — a pair can matter (THB) without being among the most traded.

**Structure (T1/T2 quarterly).** DXY against the broad and EM dollar indices (divergence = a regional move); the DXY-weighted two-year spread and the gap from spot it implies; COT percentiles for EUR, JPY, GBP, CHF, AUD, CAD and DX, leveraged funds against asset managers; the three-month cross-currency basis (widening negative = funding stress, "moves before almost anything else"); risk reversals where free; intervention footprints (MoF monthly, SNB sight deposits, the PBoC fixing gap and CNH–CNY); foreign Treasury holdings (TIC) and auction metrics; COFER in constant currency; stablecoin supply (99% dollar); the thirty-year envelopes per pair.

**Supply and demand (T3).** Balance of payments, REER against its ten-year average (the standard institutional valuation table), PPP, terms of trade for AUD/CAD/NOK/BRL, fiscal paths; the reserve question in two columns — COFER constant-currency against WGC quarterly purchases (the paper's claim that "diversification went into gold instead", invisible in COFER).

**The analyses.** Yields-up/dollar-down recurrence and persistence — **one definition shared with the Rates paper's credibility-repricing fingerprint** (front end down, term premium up, currency down, gold up; S10/S29): the two papers describe the same signature and the layer stores one series; the 2y-spread fair-value gap; carry-to-vol per pair; the dollar-cycles table (GTM-22, logged) as the chapter's long frame; the yen stack (SR-2) and hedged-yield spreads (S11) as the repatriation read; the stablecoin-dollarisation line (BRL as "the epicentre").

**Falsification tests (six, V §4).** Intervention resets positioning, not levels; Dev-2 falsified by gold falling and holding below 2024 levels while central banks turn net sellers; "the Fed is fighting, not accommodating" — the single most important test; the disorderly episode a 10–15% tail (two consecutive quarters of yields-up/dollar-down with auction deterioration falsifies it); the yuan cannot be a reserve currency this decade; the yen ~40% undervalued will appreciate; stablecoins strengthen the dollar.

**Scenarios to rewrite.** Five-to-ten-year: Dev-2 ~65–70, Dev-1 ~40 (overlapping), consolidation 10–15, disorderly 10–15, Dev-3 <5; per-pair bull/base/bear (DXY 100–110 / 92–100 / 80–90; EUR/USD, USD/JPY, GBP/USD, USD/CNY as the paper gives them).

### 4.5 Emerging markets, with China in depth (no owning paper; XXI Ch 10 and 12; III Ch 7; II S12, S15; V §3, §25–26; VI has no EM credit)

**Frame.** EM in aggregate is "a dollar-cycle instrument with a commodity overlay" and "a poor vehicle for a country view and a reasonable vehicle for a dollar view" (XXI); the dollar leads EM equity and credit by weeks and "a portfolio long EM equity, long commodities and short dollars has one position, not three" (III); contagion runs through funding channels, not trade, and the cross-currency basis fires first (II S15); the EM loop — depreciation lifts the local value of dollar debt, forced selling, further depreciation (V §3). **This is the library's gap**: no EM paper exists or is planned; the reserved XXVI "Turning Points, Bubbles and Crises" will carry the crisis mechanics (1994, 1997–98, 2001, 2015, 2018). **Ruled 10 Oct (decision 8): the EM paper is commissioned now** — "Emerging Markets: A Structural Framework", Draft 1, written by a library session on the library worktree (AQ-14), numeral assigned at the filing (XXXIV is the next free; XXVI–XXXII are Audit 2's reserved roster), in the structural-paper shape the others share: the frame (EM as a dollar-cycle instrument with a commodity overlay; the funding channel; the EM loop), the Tier 1–4 monitoring tables, the falsification tests, the scenario sets with probabilities, the Cadence Summary, Appendix D's ten questions. It draws on XXI Ch 10 and 12, III Ch 7, V §3 and §25–26, II S12 and S15, the EM shock screen (SR-46) and the vulnerability scorecard below, and leaves the crisis history to XXVI. The chapter's frame item reads the paper; its first twelve-month scenario set is the paper's.

**Structure.** EM versus DM relative (SR-14); EM hard-currency and local-currency spreads (ICE BofA EM indices on FRED); EM FX basket and the EM dollar index; the EM shock screen's quarter as the episode list (SR-46); EM portfolio flows (the IIF Capital Flows Tracker's monthly non-resident equity and debt flows by region, free); the funding channel (basis); the three-IMF-programmes-in-a-quarter re-specification rule (S15).

**China sub-chapter.** The three markets as three instruments and three risks (A-shares, Hong Kong, ADR/VIE). The model quotes the note and the stored reporting; it writes no political judgment of its own. **Scenarios to rewrite.** China ten-year (XXI 10.7): reform and discipline 20, industrial win / shareholder muddle 40, balance-sheet recession 25, rupture 15 ("not a valuation risk, so it cannot be sized by valuation"); the EM paper supplies the twelve-month set.

### 4.6 Crypto (paper IX; Thread D; I Factor V; II S4, S18, S23)

**Frame.** "Crypto is not an asset class" — eight functional types; three value theories with one measurable each (monetary premium → holder base; productive utility → fee revenue accruing *to the token*; speculative → attention); supply "perfectly predictable and completely inelastic"; price set by the marginal buyer, who in 2026 "has been the marginal *seller*"; macro liquidity "the dominant medium-term driver". **Deep tier:** BTC, ETH, SOL, ZEC, PRL (the `crypto.watch` entry, SR-10). **Light tier (ruled 10 Oct, decision 2 — four MW-6 promotions):** HYPE (Hyperliquid — the venue the system already reads for funding and OI; the operator's "Hyperlink" is read as this; fees via DS-29), TAO (the AI-token bellwether), LINK (oracles), AAVE (on-chain credit). **Category gauges, no names:** stablecoin supply by issuer and chain (S-1), tokenised treasuries and RWA, L2 value secured, privacy coins as a group, memecoin froth (S-5).

**Structure (T1/T2 quarterly).** Stablecoin supply (growth through drawdowns = bull; "sustained contraction is the most significant single bear signal"); BTC long-term-holder supply (SR-29); BTC rolling correlation to SPY/QQQ; ETH burn against issuance; RWA value and holder count; ETF flows read against float (S-2), exchange balances, OI-weighted funding (S-3; "crowded" at z > +2 with OI/mcap at a 90-day high), DVOL regime (S-4), Hyperliquid whales, miner reserves, treasury-company activity and premium to NAV; sovereign purchase announcements; the CLARITY and BIP-360/361 calendars.

**Supply and demand (T3 and the deep dives).** Issuance and unlocks (the DS-21 table); ETF and treasury-company demand; stablecoin growth as the dollar leg; the per-coin measurables — BTC: holder base, MVRV, network health, the halving row; ETH: fee capture to the token, L2 against mainnet, staking ratio and real yield; SOL: application TVL and its composition, memecoin share; ZEC: shielded supply and transaction share ("cannot be faked by exchange speculation"), the EU July 2027 headwind; PRL: its S1–S8 signposts.

## 6. The thesis register — the papers' quarterly ritual, ledgered

The papers already prescribe the ritual: quarterly, "rewrite the scenario probabilities, review the falsification tests explicitly, and state which scenario the quarter's evidence supported" (VIII, VII, V, IX, VI Cadence Summaries; IX Parts VI–VII "revisited quarterly"; the architecture's quarterly review: "Factor V adoption ledger — did evidence move?", "crypto forecast — derive from drivers *first*"). This order makes that ritual the thesis register rather than inventing a second one.

- **What a thesis is.** Per family, each quarter: the paper's scenario set with this quarter's probabilities (the 12-month set), and a 3-month thesis stated as a measurable (a return sign and magnitude, a level, a spread, a ratio) with a falsifier and the Book it informs. Both written **from drivers before looking at the prior** ("a range that drifts with spot is following price, not forecasting it"), vintage-stored.
- **Where it lives.** Entries in the probability ledger under report key `alt_asset`: "a probability may print only if it was first written to the probability ledger … with its resolution criterion declared at the same moment"; weights sum to one; Brier-scored by code against the coin's 0.25; the grader scores the ordering and direction of scenario sets, the level of the 3-month measurable. No new grader.
- **The opener.** Each chapter opens with last quarter's theses graded — hit, miss, not yet due — before the new ones are written; the 12-month set is re-graded each quarter until due; the falsification-test lines print beside them.
- **The first set** is seeded by hand at Doctrine #2 (7–8 Nov) for 1Q27 and for the year, carried in the 1 Dec Monthly's slow layers as a table, graded in the January edition. From January, theses live in the Quarterly.
- **Every major thesis states** market expectation, the Chester read, the difference, the evidence basis, and what changes the view (§26.12), and a tactical trade against a chapter's structural view is tagged `counter-structural` in the register and sized smaller (the Reader's Guide, "From the library to the trade").
