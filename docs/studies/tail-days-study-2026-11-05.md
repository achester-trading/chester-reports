Eight of the ten best days sit within a week of a worst day because they are the same phenomenon: a short-gamma, forced-flow regime in which the market overshoots in both directions. The best days are the rebound leg — short covering, re-leveraging by the same mechanical sellers, and in every completed episode a policy response. They are not independent opportunities to be "captured"; they are conditional on having been in the worst-day regime.

## 2. Episode notes: catalysts and the conditions beforehand

### A. 26 Dec 2018 (+5.0%)
- **Catalyst on the day:** none scheduled. Christmas-Eve capitulation (24 Dec, thin tape) after the 19 Dec FOMC hike and "autopilot" balance-sheet remark; Mnuchin's weekend calls to bank CEOs; a large month-end pension rebalance into equities was known in advance; Powell's "patient" pivot followed on 4 Jan.
- **Conditions before (approx.):** S&P ~20% below the 20 Sep high at the 24 Dec low; VIX ~36 at the low with the term structure inverted; HY OAS up ~200bp in the quarter to ~530–540bp; breadth washed out (single-digit % of stocks above the 50-day); systematic de-leveraging largely complete.
- **Type:** capitulation bottom + known mechanical bid + policy pivot pending. The bounce day was anticipatable as a regime (not the date) from oversold breadth, HY acceleration already slowing, and the rebalance calendar.

### B. March–June 2020
- **2 Mar +4.4:** G7/Fed-cut hopes after a −12% week (24–28 Feb) that took VIX from ~14 to ~40. The emergency 50bp cut on 3 Mar then sold off — the first "policy that reveals severity" day.
- **9 Mar −7.8:** weekend Saudi–Russia oil price war (WTI −25%) on top of the virus; 10y briefly below 0.5%; first circuit breaker. Unscheduled, exogenous.
- **10 Mar +4.8:** stimulus hopes. **11 Mar −5.1:** WHO pandemic declaration, travel ban that evening. **12 Mar −9.6:** ECB disappointment, Fed's $1.5tn repo expansion at midday failed to hold the tape — liquidity, not rates, was the problem.
- **13 Mar +9.0:** national emergency declaration, fiscal package talk (Friday). **16 Mar −12.3:** Sunday-night Fed to zero + $700bn QE read as panic; Treasury market dysfunction (basis-trade unwind, off-the-run spreads), FRA-OIS and CP spreads blowing out, dollar squeeze; worst day since 1987.
- **17 Mar +5.8:** CPFF/PDCF facilities. **18 Mar −5.7:** everything sold including Treasuries and gold — the pure de-leveraging day.
- **23 Mar (not on the list):** unlimited QE + corporate credit facilities = the low (2,191 intraday). **24 Mar +9.5:** biggest day since 1933. **26 Mar +6.2:** CARES Act passes the Senate on the day jobless claims print 3.3m — bad news ignored is the regime-change tell.
- **1 Apr −4.6** ("painful two weeks") then **6 Apr +7.2** (curve-flattening evidence NY/Italy).
- **11 Jun −6.0:** post-FOMC (10 Jun dour outlook), second-wave headlines, after a 45% rally with retail froth (airlines, Hertz) and VIX already ~27 → 40. Scheduled catalyst, crowded tape.
- **Conditions before the episode (approx.):** 19 Feb all-time high with VIX ~14, record concentration and complacency, very low put/call, vol-control and risk-parity leverage near maximum, dealers long gamma at the high and violently short below ~3,200. HY OAS went 350 → ~1,090bp by 23 Mar. ES top-of-book depth fell ~90%.
- **Type:** exogenous shock into a maximally complacent, maximally levered tape; every bounce day was a policy day; the crash days were either policy disappointments or policy that confirmed severity. The 16 Mar lesson: a rate cut does nothing when the plumbing is broken — the right tells were funding and Treasury-market dysfunction, not the Fed funds rate.

### C. 10 Nov 2022 (+5.7%)
- **Catalyst:** scheduled — October CPI 7.7% vs 7.9% expected, core 0.3%. 2y yields fell ~25bp; Nasdaq +7.4%.
- **Conditions before (approx.):** 25% bear market to the 12 Oct low, which itself printed a −2%/+2.6% intraday reversal on the September CPI; positioning extremely light (BofA FMS cash ~6.3%, CTAs short, hedge funds net short, heavy put ownership); VIX ~26 and falling; 1-day implied move on CPI ~2%.
- **Type:** scheduled-event squeeze on washed-out positioning with rates as the driver. The event-vol ledger (item 101) plus the crowding/positioning gauges are the anticipation tools; the market had already shown the reversal pattern a month earlier.

### D. 3–9 April 2025
- **Catalysts:** scheduled — reciprocal tariffs announced after the close on 2 Apr at multiples of what was priced (3 Apr −5.0); China's 34% retaliation (4 Apr −5.9); then 7 Apr's false "pause" headline produced an ~8% intraday round trip (the fragility tell); 8–9 Apr the bond market broke — 10y ~3.9% → ~4.5% in three sessions, 30y above 5%, swap spreads collapsing, basis-trade unwind, "sell America" — and the 90-day pause at 1:18pm on 9 Apr delivered +9.6%, record volume, the largest one-day short covering on record.
- **Conditions before (approx.):** S&P peaked 19 Feb, ~8–10% below it on 2 Apr; VIX ~21–22 (elevated, not extreme); hedge-fund gross leverage at record highs, multi-strategy crowding, the basis trade near record size; HY OAS ~260bp → ~460bp by 7 Apr; VIX 45 → ~60 intraday 7 Apr.
- **Type:** own-policy shock, reversed by the policy's author when the Treasury market forced it. The policy put was triggered by rates and funding, not by equities — the same lesson as 16 Mar 2020, from the other side.

### E. 5 Nov 2026 (−4.4%)
- **Catalysts:** OpenAI leadership discussing lower 2027 data-center capacity expectations after the midterms; voter-approved local data-center restrictions with newly elected governors in Texas, Ohio and Georgia backing construction pauses; Goldman's estimate that up to 45% of planned US capacity could face temporary moratoria; peak-capex forecasts pulled forward to 2027; CoreWeave debt yields at 17%. Nasdaq −5.4% intraday into correction (−11% from the 6 Oct record), S&P −4.1%, SOX nearly −10%, Nvidia −9% erasing its 2026 gains, AMD −16%, Micron −15%. Mechanical amplifiers: MS QDS estimated $52bn of levered-ETF rebalancing supply into the close (second only to $55bn on 5 Jun 2026); hedge funds dumping the hyperscaler shares bought in Wednesday's post-election rally; long-only "frozen".
- **Conditions before:** October was the Nasdaq's worst month in over a year (~$2tn wiped out on AI-spending fears; Intel/Amazon disappointments 29–30 Oct; "worst day since June" on 30 Oct); 22 Oct −2.6% on AI-capex fears; levered-ETF AUM at records (SOXL inflows on every dip, so the $-per-1% sensitivity was at a record); 4 Nov: index up while SPHB/SPLV fell 3.4% (item 100's divergence); midterm 1-day implied move only ~1.2% (item 101's "cheap event vol"); 30y at ~5.6% with the Fed (Musalem) saying more firming is needed.
- **Type:** scheduled political catalyst (midterms) converting an already-deteriorating AI narrative into a forced-flow day. The distinguishing feature versus A–D: **the policy put is not available in the usual form.** The Fed is tightening, the political shock is structural (local moratoria are not "paused" by a tweet), and the credit tell (CoreWeave 17%) is a private-credit problem the Fed does not own. A mechanical bounce can still print (short covering, levered-ETF re-leveraging on any up day, dealer gamma), but the policy-day catalyst that produced every best day in A–D is missing. Candidate catalysts for a bounce are corporate, not governmental: hyperscaler capex reaffirmation, an OpenAI clarification, Nvidia's late-November print, the 11 Nov MSCI review already under watch.

## 3. Cross-episode findings

1. **The day is never forecastable; the regime always was.** Twenty of twenty tail days had an identifiable catalyst that no metric could have timed. In every completed episode the market was already in an elevated-vol, negative-gamma, forced-flow regime at least a day before the worst days (the first-day shocks — 9 Mar 2020, 3 Apr 2025 — came with VIX already 2–3x its February level and crowding at records). Anticipation means scoring fragility, not predicting dates.
2. **Both tails come from the same four mechanical sellers/buyers:** dealer gamma hedging, levered-ETF rebalancing, vol-control/vol-target funds, and trend followers (plus risk parity when bonds fail to hedge). They sell into weakness and are forced back in on strength — which is why the best days sit next to the worst days. The stack estimates one of the four from item 98 and the dealer piece from 6b; the vol-control and CTA pieces are not estimated anywhere.
3. **Every completed best day was a policy day or a known mechanical bid.** 26 Dec 2018 (rebalance + pivot pending), all seven 2020 rebounds (Fed/fiscal), 10 Nov 2022 (CPI → pivot hope), 9 Apr 2025 (pause). The question for any new worst day is therefore "is a policy response available, and what triggers it?" — and in 2020 and 2025 the trigger was the Treasury/funding market, not equities.
4. **Credit and plumbing led the policy response; equities did not.** HY acceleration, FRA-OIS/CP spreads and Treasury-market dysfunction (basis trade, swap spreads, off-the-run) were the variables that forced the Fed in 2020 and the White House in 2025. The stack has HY Spread Acceleration, Liquidity & Funding Stress, SR-36 (basis-trade proxy), MOVE-vs-VIX and the 10y attribution — the pieces exist but are not composed into a "policy-put trigger" read.
5. **Scheduled catalysts dominate the post-2020 list.** 11 Jun 2020 (FOMC), 10 Nov 2022 (CPI), 2 Apr 2025 (announcement), 3 Nov 2026 (midterms). Item 101's event-vol ledger is the right tool and 5 Nov 2026 is the first entry where "cheap event vol" preceded a tail day.
6. **Liquidity depth is the amplifier every time** (ES top-of-book collapse in 2020; the 7 Apr 2025 fake-headline round trip; 5 Nov 2026 "long-only frozen"). Nothing in the stack measures it directly.
7. **The Morningstar lesson is the wrong lesson for a trader.** "Don't miss the best days" ignores that the best days are inseparable from the worst. The vol-managed result (Moreira & Muir 2017 and successors): scaling exposure inversely to recent realized variance — stepping down in exactly these regimes, missing both tails — improves risk-adjusted returns. That is a Book A rule candidate, and for Book C / DTH-1 it means size, not direction, is the first variable to change after a −4% day.
8. **2026 differs on the policy axis.** A–D had an available policy put; E does not, in the usual form. Base rates built on A–D (e.g., "a +4% day within a week") should be conditioned on policy-put availability before they are applied to this episode.

## 5. Proposed rulings — item 106 (a–g)

**106a — Two-sided tail base-rate table (XX).** From S&P daily data since 1950 (and the Morningstar window separately): after a bottom-decile day, P(top-decile day within 1/5/10 days); distribution of next 1/5/20-day returns and absolute ranges; conditional on VIX regime (from 1990) and on whether a policy response followed within 5 days (hand-tagged from 1987). Rights: base-rate rows; sizing input to 106g. ~4h.

**106b — Mechanical flow stack (extends item 98).** Add three estimators beside levered-ETF flow and dealer hedging: (i) vol-control exposure — target-vol model (10–12% target, blended 1m/3m realized) → implied equity weight → daily Δ-flow on an assumed AUM; (ii) CTA trend proxy — multi-horizon moving-average signals on ES/NQ/RTY/10y with flip levels in price terms; (iii) risk-parity leverage proxy from stock-bond realized vol and correlation. Output: one daily "forced flow" line in $bn with sign, printed at 15:00 and 16:45 with item 98, and the flip levels in the 07:00 anchor beside the dealer levels. Backtestable from price data. Rights: report line + flag; sizing input to 106g. ~15h.

**106c — Tail-day fragility composite.** Daily condition score from existing series: VIX level and VIX/VIX3M inversion; VVIX; GEX sign and distance to flip; HY 20-day acceleration; funding-stress overlay; crowding-unwind; trend-breadth; dispersion/correlation; MOVE-vs-VIX; levered-ETF $-per-1% sensitivity; event-calendar density (from 101). Calibrated as P(|return| ≥ 3% within 10 days | score decile) back to 1990 on the price-based subset; dealer pieces join from Sep 2026 with their own ledger. Rights: daily close line; a *size* modifier for Book C / DTH-1 (half size in the top decile) — never a direction right. ~12h + ledger.

**106d — Policy-put trigger panel.** Compose the existing pieces into one read with two parts: *triggers* (10y 3-day change, swap spreads, SR-36 basis stress, off-the-run spread, MOVE; FRA-OIS/SOFR-IORB, CP spreads, cross-currency basis; HY > 500bp with acceleration; IG primary-market shutdown days) and *availability* (Fed stance from the Warsh matrix — easing/hold/hiking; inflation room; whether the shock is the administration's own policy and therefore reversible; emergency-meeting odds from the PM layer). Printed in plumbing & rates on any day the fragility composite is in its top quintile. Rights: report line + a "policy-put unavailable" flag that conditions 106a's base rates. ~8h.

**106e — Depth / price-impact proxy.** Confirm what the liquidity-fragility panel carries; if no intraday measure, add an Amihud-style price-impact ratio on SPY/ES (|return| per $ volume, 5-min bars from Massive), its 20-day percentile, and the SPY top-of-book spread where Massive provides it. Rights: tape-section line; input to 106c. ~3h if missing.

**106f — Catalyst-class tags on the vol-event ledger (item 52) and event-vol ledger (item 101).** Tag each tail day: scheduled/unscheduled; class (policy shock, policy response, exogenous, data print, positioning unwind, earnings, political). Enter the twenty Morningstar days by hand as the seed rows. ~1h by hand.

**106g — Vol-managed sizing rule (Book A candidate; DTH-1 rule).** Backtest exposure ∝ 1/realized variance (capped at 1.5x, floored at 0.25x) on the Book A benchmark ledgers since 1990 and present as a Book A rule candidate for Doctrine #2; for DTH-1, a standing rule: after any bottom-decile day, position size halves and stops widen to the 5-day ATR until the fragility composite leaves its top decile. ~6h.

**106h — FlashAlpha Alpha-month replay (schedules work, not a build).** FlashAlpha's archive covers SPY from 3 Jan 2017 to 4 Nov 2026 — all twenty dates — but historical analytics are Alpha-tier. The December one-month upgrade should be **Alpha, not Growth**, and its first job is this replay: exposure summary, GEX by strike, volatility surface and 0DTE share for each of the twenty dates ±5 sessions, written to `docs/studies/tail-days-dealer-replay.md`. Reminder already set for 16 and 30 Nov. ~3h during the Alpha month.

Total: ~51h of sessions + ~1h by hand now + ~3h in December. Recommendation: accept 106a–h; build order 106a → 106b → 106c → 106d, with 106e–g alongside; none of it ahead of the ruled Category 1 work.
