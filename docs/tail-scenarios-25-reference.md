# The 25 Tail Scenarios — Reference List

*Source: Chester Reports architecture, Part 16. Prepared as input for white-paper
drafting.*

**Framing note.** These are not black swans. Anything on a list is priceable by
definition; the genuine tail is absent from any list that could be written. These
are better described as **high-impact, underpriced tail risks** — scenarios with
identifiable mechanisms, named first-mover series, and low base rates.

**Effective n is approximately zero for all 25.** They are conditioners, never
triggers. A tripwire firing changes the language of a report and tightens a
related predicate; it does not generate a trade.

**Organizing structure.** Scenarios 1–20 are largely shocks to the world.
Scenarios 21–25 form a distinct class: **failures of a price-insensitive
buyer** — sovereign funds, passive flows, stablecoin issuers, investment-grade
mandates, ETF authorized participants. That class is arguably more dangerous,
because standard macro indicators say nothing about it and the withdrawal is
mechanical rather than sentiment-driven: it does not reverse when things look
cheap.

## Tier A — daily cadence (fast-moving; first-mover series update intraday)

**1 · Funding-market accident.**
Reserves fall below the threshold at which dealers ration balance sheet; repo
rates spike; leveraged positions unwind into a thin market. The 2019 repo episode
occurred at higher reserve levels than currently prevail, with the reverse repo
facility now effectively exhausted and the Treasury General Account draining
reserves further.
*First mover:* SOFR–IORB spread, standing repo facility usage, settlement fails.

**2 · Yen carry unwind.**
The yen is the world's funding currency and Japan the largest foreign holder of
Treasuries. A faster-than-expected Bank of Japan normalization, or a failed
intervention, forces a global de-leveraging as carry positions financed in yen
are unwound simultaneously.
*First mover:* USD/JPY intramonth path divergence, JGB 30-year, cross-asset
correlation spike.

**7 · Chokepoint disruption–price divergence resolves.**
*Re-specified: the closure is a current condition, not a tail.* Physical
disruption at crisis extremes coexisting with commodity prices at pre-crisis
levels. Two readings — adaptation (shadow fleets, rerouting, demand destruction
absorbed it) or mispricing (a chokepoint that has failed repeatedly underwritten
at normal premiums). The tail is the divergence closing violently.
*First mover:* transit counts as a ratio to pre-crisis baseline, against crude.

**19 · LDI-style forced deleveraging.**
Any large allocator using leverage against long-duration liabilities is exposed
to a rate gap. The 2022 gilt episode was a preview: collateral calls force
selling of the very assets whose price is falling.
*First mover:* long-end realized volatility, term premium spikes, cross-asset
correlation.

**20 · Index concentration accident.**
Mechanical rather than fundamental. As top-ten index weight rises, a single-name
failure becomes an index event — and passive flows amplify rather than absorb it,
because index funds must sell proportionally regardless of view.
*First mover:* single-name gamma on the largest constituents, top-ten weight,
implied correlation.

## Tier B — weekly cadence

**3 · AI capex halt.**
One hyperscaler cutting capital expenditure guidance is sufficient. Circularity
in the buildout — suppliers taking stakes in customers, vendor financing,
prepayment structures — means a single cut propagates simultaneously through chip
orders, neocloud debt, data-center asset-backed securities and power contracts.
*First mover:* supplier inventory and forward purchase commitments, hyperscaler
capex as a share of operating cash flow, Korean semiconductor export data.

**4 · Stablecoin depeg at scale.**
No longer a crypto event. Issuers hold Treasury bill reserves as a matter of law,
and official financing plans increasingly count on their demand to absorb bill
issuance. A depeg forces reserve liquidation into the bill market.
*First mover:* aggregate stablecoin supply, issuer reserve attestations, bill
yields.

**5 · European sovereign fragmentation.**
Peripheral and semi-core spread widening beyond the point where the European
Central Bank's willingness to intervene is tested. The only euro-area channel
that reliably becomes a global event.
*First mover:* OAT–Bund and BTP–Bund spreads.

**6 · Private credit mark event.**
Marks are quarterly, the underlying is floating-rate, and the borrowers are the
most refinancing-exposed cohort in credit. Business development company share
prices mark daily and therefore lead the reported marks.
*First mover:* BDC price-to-net-asset-value, CCC spreads, payment-in-kind income
share.

**9 · Failed Treasury auction.**
Not literal failure, but a tail wide enough to force reassessment of foreign
demand. Made more consequential by bill-heavy issuance and elevated near-term
maturity share.
*First mover:* auction tails, bid-to-cover, indirect bidder share, term premium.

**14 · Commercial real estate credit cascade.**
Regional bank concentration, a maturity wall, and office marks that have not
cleared. The transmission runs through bank capital rather than through property
prices directly.
*First mover:* CMBS spreads by rating tier, regional bank options skew, bank CRE
delinquencies.

**23 · Stablecoin regulatory reversal.** *(price-insensitive buyer class)*
The mirror of scenario 4. Not a depeg but a rule change — forced reserve
divestment, issuance restriction, or licensing withdrawal. If issuers are the
marginal bill buyer, a regulatory reversal removes that bid directly and by
decree.
*First mover:* legislative and rulemaking calendar, issuer reserve composition.

**25 · ETF wrapper liquidity failure.** *(price-insensitive buyer class)*
Not a fund failing — the authorized-participant arbitrage mechanism failing under
stress in less liquid underlyings: high yield, bank loans, emerging market debt,
private credit. The wrapper promises intraday liquidity the underlying cannot
deliver.
*First mover:* premium and discount to net asset value in high-yield ETFs,
creation-redemption activity.

## Tier C — monthly cadence

**10 · Fed independence event.**
Appointments, political pressure, or a policy decision markets read as
politically rather than economically driven. Prices in the term premium and the
currency rather than at the front end — the front end reflects expected policy;
the long end reflects trust in the institution setting it.
*First mover:* breakevens versus term premium divergence, dollar, gold's
speculative bid.

**11 · Japanese repatriation from the domestic side.**
Distinct from a carry unwind. If Japanese life insurers and pension funds
repatriate because domestic yields finally compensate on an unhedged basis, that
is a structural withdrawal of the largest foreign bid for Treasuries — slow,
persistent, and not reversible by intervention.
*First mover:* Treasury International Capital data for Japan, JGB 30-year,
hedged-yield spreads.

**12 · China property or local-government financing vehicle event.**
Exports deflation through goods prices, applies currency pressure, and delivers a
commodity demand shock simultaneously. Three transmission channels from one
event.
*First mover:* renminbi, copper-to-gold ratio, Chinese export prices.

**15 · Emerging market debt crisis with a dollar squeeze.**
Dollar strength combined with refinancing walls in frontier sovereigns. The
IMF-programme wave is the slow-burn version; a disorderly version compounds
through cross-currency funding markets.
*First mover:* EM sovereign spreads, dollar path, cross-currency basis.

**16 · Inflation reacceleration forcing hikes.**
Tariff pass-through, an energy floor, and services stickiness combining. **The
scenario most frameworks are least positioned for**, because contemporary
analysis is generally built on an assumed disinflation path — which makes the
asymmetry itself a reason to weight it.
*First mover:* sticky core CPI, five-year five-year forward inflation, policy
path dispersion.

**17 · Insurance or reinsurance capital event.**
A very large catastrophe, or several correlated ones, forcing general accounts to
sell long-duration assets into a falling market. Insurance capital is a
significant and under-monitored holder of corporate and structured credit.
*First mover:* catastrophe bond spreads, reinsurer equity, January renewal
pricing.

**21 · Sovereign wealth fund forced selling.** *(price-insensitive buyer class)*
A large reserve manager or sovereign fund liquidating to fund a domestic deficit
— a persistent oil price collapse is the usual trigger. These are among the
largest price-insensitive holders of global equities, and when they turn, they
turn slowly and relentlessly rather than in a single event.
*First mover:* oil against Gulf fiscal breakevens, sovereign fund disclosures.

**22 · Passive-flow reversal.** *(price-insensitive buyer class)*
Demographic decumulation exceeding contribution flows, or a change to retirement
default treatment. Passive is the largest price-insensitive bid in equities, and
its withdrawal is mechanical — it does not reverse when valuations look
attractive.
*First mover:* fund flow data, net passive creation, retirement account net
flows.

**24 · BBB downgrade cascade.** *(price-insensitive buyer class)*
Roughly half of investment grade sits at the BBB boundary. Downgrade to high
yield triggers mandate-driven forced selling, and a modest wave overwhelms high
yield's capacity to absorb it. Structurally identical to tail-tier credit stress,
at far larger notional.
*First mover:* BBB share of investment grade, ratings action counts, BBB–BB
spread.

## Tier D — quarterly cadence

**8 · Taiwan.**
Low probability, near-unbounded impact. Removes the semiconductor supply chain
and the artificial intelligence trade simultaneously, alongside the shipping and
insurance consequences of a regional conflict.
*First mover:* Taiwan export orders, foundry revenue, semiconductor equities,
shipping and war-risk insurance rates.

**18 · Cryptographic break in settlement infrastructure.**
Not primarily a cryptocurrency question. A break in the cryptography underlying
settlement, custody and clearing systems. **The tracked variable is the gap
between capability milestones and post-quantum migration progress** — the threat
is bounded by migration speed, not by raw capability.
*First mover:* post-quantum standard adoption in settlement, custody and banking
infrastructure, measured against verified capability milestones.

## Documented blind spot — no tripwire

**13 · Cyberattack on financial infrastructure.**
A central depository, major exchange, or systemically important custodian.
**Price-based indicators fail by construction here:** settlement stops before
prices move, so the usual leading indicators are silent precisely when the event
occurs.

Retained on the list with **no tripwire assigned**, deliberately. Attaching a
weak proxy would be worse than documenting the gap — it would create false
confidence in a domain where the failure mode is invisibility. Reviewed each
quarter as an open instrumentation problem.

## Design principles worth carrying into the white paper

1. **Cadence follows speed, not importance.** Taiwan is among the
   highest-impact scenarios and is checked quarterly, because its first-mover
   series only updates quarterly. Checking it daily would be theatre.
2. **Every scenario names a first-mover series.** A scenario without a tripwire is
   commentary, not risk management.
3. **Elevated is a state, not a call.** A tripwire firing changes analytical
   language and tightens a related forecast; it does not generate a position.
4. **All are conditioners.** With effective n near zero, treating any tripwire as
   a trading signal would be the most severe overfitting available.
5. **Blind spots stay on the list.** An acknowledged gap is more useful than a
   weak proxy, because it remains visible at every review.
6. **Scenarios get re-specified as the world changes.** Scenario 7 was originally
   written as a closure risk; the closure occurred, so the scenario became the
   divergence between physical disruption and price. A tail list that never gets
   rewritten is a list that has stopped being read.
