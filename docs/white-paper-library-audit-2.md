# White Paper Library — Audit 2: The Corpus as a System

**Question:** Is this library, taken as one integrated intellectual system, a comprehensive, rigorous, coherent, practical, forward-looking education in global markets, investing, trading, risk, and market behavior — worth the 348,000 words it now runs to? And if not yet, what would make it so?
**Corpus:** `achester-trading/chester-reports` @ `f92f81b` (7 Sep 2026) — 24 papers, 348,432 words, plus the library guide
**Method:** zero-based. A curriculum was designed first, without reference to the corpus (Part 1). The corpus was then mapped against it using three kinds of evidence: the Part/Chapter structure of every paper; a coverage matrix that probed all 24 papers for every participant, edge, horizon, environment, behavioral driver, "what might change" topic, and curriculum subject in the brief; and sampled close reading of the load-bearing passages (the Doctrine's premise and small-capital sections, the Monthly manual's epistemic position and chapter anatomy, the Positioning participant map, the Tail paper's mutation scan). Where a judgment below rests on structure and probes rather than close reading, it says so; close reading of every paper is the edit phase's job, and this audit tells it where to look.
**Predecessor:** Audit 1 (hygiene and consistency) is closed; nothing here re-litigates it.

## Part 0 — The verdict

**As it stands, the library is not yet the thing you described. It is the strongest half of it.**

What it is: an unusually practical, unusually honest set of mechanism papers. The regions where it is deep are deeper than anything a reader will find in a textbook or a bank research library — dealer hedging and the gamma complex (three papers, ~50,000 words, with worked figures); the rate-and-liquidity machine and its plumbing; corporate credit as a cycle-timing tool; the variance risk premium and volatility regimes; the base rates of ordinary markets; and, above all, an operating doctrine that states the operator's rules, the four books, and the small-capital advantage more precisely than most professionals ever state their own. The papers are consistent with each other (Audit 1), argued from mechanism, and honest about their limits. That is rare and it is the reason the rest is worth doing.

What it is not yet, and why — five structural facts, each measured:

1. **It has no foundational layer.** The corpus teaches mechanisms — how dealers hedge, how credit leads equities, how gold broke its model — without first teaching the theory that makes them one subject: how a price forms, what it is a price *of* (expectations, not facts), who the participants are and what each is forced to do, and why an opportunity can exist at all. The theory of edge, which is the intellectual center of the entire enterprise, occupies one paragraph in the Doctrine (§2.2, eight sources of alpha in nine lines). Across 348,000 words: "behavioral edge" 0 hits, "structural edge" 0, "horizon edge" 0, "informational edge" 1, "why does this edge exist / competed away" 9, "capacity" of an edge 1 sentence. The six questions in your brief — why is there an opportunity, why should it exist, who is on the other side, why might they be forced, why hasn't competition removed it, what would end it — are asked nowhere as a discipline, though individual papers answer some of them in passing.

2. **The horizon ladder is barbell-shaped.** Intraday and daily mechanics (Daily Cascade, Dealer's Hand, Volatility Ch. 10, Options) run to ~60,000 words; generational and secular content (Foundations, Currencies, Base Rates Part IV) to ~40,000. The weeks-to-months horizon — Book B's horizon, the one where a discretionary individual most plausibly competes — is barely named: "weeks" as a horizon 9 hits, "months" 10, "secular" 5. There is no paper on what moves price over weeks, who competes there, and what edge a small book has at that rung.

3. **The "timeless" papers carry dated dispatches inside them.** Foundations' five factors each carry a "present moment — a dispatch" and 2028/2036 scenarios; the Twenty-Five carries 25 per-scenario treatments written against the Monthly's Part 16; every asset-class paper carries forecast ranges as of August 2026; Tops and Bottoms closes with "Today Against History (May 2026)"; three papers still disagree about gold's August move and HY spreads (Audit 1, B1–B2). The perishable content is roughly 30,000 words and it is what will make the library read as stale in eighteen months — not the mechanisms.

4. **Environments and regimes are everywhere and therefore nowhere.** Regime identification appears in Volatility Part I, Portfolio Construction Part I, Doctrine Part V, Technicals Ch. 2, and as a "Regime Dependence" sub-section in every Monthly-manual pillar — five treatments, no single one. Of the eighteen environments in your brief, four are close to absent as subjects: banking crises (6 mentions, none mechanistic), liquidity crises as a mechanism (5), bubbles as an anatomy (8; Minsky/Kindleberger 6), technological revolutions as a market regime (2). The long debt cycle — your item 7 — is present as Foundations Factor IV, Currencies' Dalio chapter, Monthly Pillar 8 and Base Rates Part IV, but nowhere as one argument about where in the cycle the reader is standing and what that changes about every other paper.

5. **Market papers and system papers are interleaved.** The Daily Cascade paper and the Systematic Book are system papers and know it. The Monthly Macro manual is a hybrid: its ten pillar chapters each carry a genuinely good macro education (The 101, Predictive Properties, How Institutions Use It, Tradability, Failure Modes, Regime Dependence) interleaved with report mechanics (The Metrics, Data Mechanics, composite thresholds). Foundations and the Twenty-Five are report companions carrying market content; Tops and Bottoms is a market-history study carrying the T&B report's methodology. A reader who wants to understand markets, and a reader who wants to operate your system, currently read the same pages.

**The standard.** Your brief sets it correctly: not "is this good educational material" but "does a sophisticated reader who studies it carefully develop an integrated mental model, regime recognition, a sense of where edge realistically lives, risk instincts, market history, the ability to translate research into trades, breadth beyond his existing models, depth to mechanisms, and the first-principles capacity to reason about conditions that have never occurred." Against that standard the corpus today scores high on mechanism depth, on risk instincts, and on translation-to-trades in its strongest papers; it scores low on the integrated model (no foundational layer), on regime recognition as a unified skill, on breadth beyond the mechanisms you have already asked about, and on first-principles reasoning about novel conditions (the Tail paper's Part 3 mutation scan is the one place that does this deliberately, and it is excellent — it should be a discipline, not a chapter).

**Can it become the coherent operating system you describe?** Yes, and without starting over. About 70% of the current word count survives the restructure as-is or with a standard tactical/wiring chapter added. The work is: write four foundational papers the corpus is missing; split two hybrids; move ~30,000 words of dated content into appendices that the reports maintain; expand two papers (Tops and Bottoms into turning points and bubbles; Earnings into events and expectations); add a standard tactical-applications chapter and a standard wiring chapter to every content paper; and install the annual structural review as a mechanism rather than an intention. Part 7 sequences it.

## Part 1 — The curriculum, built from scratch

Designed before re-reading the corpus, as the brief asked. This is what a practical education in global markets, investing, and trading would contain if it were built for one sophisticated individual operating a $300,000 multi-horizon book with discretion plus systematic monitoring, and no mandate.

### Layer 0 — How to think about markets (foundations)

**0.1 What a price is.** A price is the expectation of the marginal participant, weighted by their capital and their urgency. Markets move on the difference between what happens and what was expected, not on whether news is good. Price discovery as a process, not an outcome. The market as a participant: price changes positioning, fundamentals (through financing, hiring, capex, collateral), narratives, and therefore subsequent price. Reflexivity as the general case, efficiency as the special case.

**0.2 Time.** The horizon ladder — intraday, days, weeks, months, cyclical, secular, generational — and, for each rung: what actually moves price at that horizon; which participants compete there and with what resources; what is knowable and what is noise at that rung; what edge a small book plausibly has; how horizons couple (the secular view sets the prior for the cyclical; the cyclical sets the regime for the weekly; the weekly sets the setup for the day). Horizon itself as a source of edge: the willingness to wait, and the ability to change horizon without asking.

**0.3 Participants and constraints.** The ecology: retail, hedge funds, mutual funds, pensions, insurers, sovereign wealth funds, banks, dealers, market makers, corporations, governments, central banks, CTAs, vol-control, risk parity, passive, private equity, producers and consumers, arbitrageurs, systematic funds, liquidity providers, leveraged investors. For each: what they must hold and why (mandate, liability, regulation, benchmark, calendar); what they are forced to do and when; where their constraints create predictable behavior; who owns the market, and who trades it. "Who is on the other side" as a habit.

**0.4 The theory of edge.** Why opportunities exist in a competitive market: informational, analytical, behavioral, structural, liquidity, horizon, positioning/flow, event-driven, cross-asset, regime-recognition, execution, patience/optionality. For each: the mechanism that creates it, who has it, its capacity (how much money it can absorb before it disappears), what competes it away, and how it dies. Durable performance from several partially independent edges, none of them a forecasting model. The six questions as a checklist for every thesis. The small-book advantage stated precisely, and its shadow.

**0.5 Epistemics.** Base rates before theses. What a number means; standard errors; effective sample size; regime as sample size; how to fool yourself with a ledger; Bayes in sentences; process quality versus outcome quality; distinguishing knowledge (mechanism), regularity (empirical, regime-dependent), and forecast (dated, low confidence); knowing when a relationship has stopped working.

**0.6 History as the laboratory.** Not one country's equity bears, but the full set: bubbles and manias (anatomy, Minsky–Kindleberger, how they end, how to hold and how to trade them); crashes with and without recessions; banking crises as a mechanism (runs, franchise value, mark-to-market versus held-to-maturity, the 2023 template); liquidity crises and the dash for cash; sovereign and currency crises (1994, 1997–98, 2011, 2015, 2022); structural breaks and the relationships that died (curve inversion, gold–real rates, stock–bond correlation). Each episode read for who was forced, what the tell was, and what the frameworks of the day got wrong.

### Layer 1 — The macro machine

**1.1 Growth, inflation, employment.** The business cycle and its measurement; the data calendar; expectations versus realized; surprises and revisions; what each release moves and why; how the same print reads differently in different regimes.

**1.2 Money, rates, and the plumbing.** Central banks and their toolkits; the rate-and-liquidity machine; reserves, repo, the Treasury's account, money funds, dealer balance sheets; Treasury issuance and the fiscal-monetary interaction; transmission lags; what breaks in the plumbing and how it shows.

**1.3 Debt cycles and where we stand.** The short debt cycle and the long one; deleveragings (deflationary and inflationary); financial repression, fiscal dominance, yield-curve control, capital controls; sovereign crises; the diagnostic questions for locating the present in the cycle; and the honest statement of what the last hundred years cannot tell you about the next twenty.

**1.4 The global system.** The dollar and its alternatives; balance of payments; capital flows; exchange-rate regimes; reserve management and diversification; international transmission; geopolitics as an economic force — sanctions, economic warfare, fragmentation, reglobalization.

**1.5 Regimes and environments.** The eighteen environments — expansion, recession, inflation, disinflation, deflation, stagnation, bubble, crash, liquidity crisis, banking crisis, fiscal crisis, currency crisis, war, technological revolution, financial repression, capital controls, monetary-system change, structural break — as a matrix against asset classes and signals: what each asset does, which signals work and which invert, which participants dominate, what the base rates are. Regime identification and transition as a skill; structural breaks as the case where the matrix itself changes.

### Layer 2 — Asset classes and instruments

For each: what it is and what it prices; who holds it and why; supply, demand, and the marginal participant; the curve or term structure; valuation and its limits; the instruments and their traps; failure modes; behavior across the environments; and tactical applications by horizon.

2.1 Government bonds and rates · 2.2 Corporate credit, loans, private credit · 2.3 Equities (index machine, factors, sectors, concentration, single names, valuation) · 2.4 International equities · 2.5 Currencies · 2.6 Commodities: energy, metals, agriculture · 2.7 Digital assets · 2.8 Real assets: real estate, infrastructure, and insurance-linked risk transfer · 2.9 Volatility as an asset · 2.10 Derivatives: futures, options from the inside (expression) and the outside (dealer inventory), swaps, structured products · 2.11 Private markets and alternatives.

### Layer 3 — Market structure and behavior (how price actually forms)

**3.1 Microstructure and plumbing of trading.** Order books, auctions (open and close), dealers and non-bank market makers, high-frequency and automated liquidity, ETF creation/redemption and what it does to the underlying, settlement, clearing, collateral, margin, financing — and the cascades that run through them.
**3.2 Positioning and flows.** Mechanical holders and their calendars; absorption; estimating unseen flows; reading positioning without misreading it.
**3.3 Derivatives-driven dynamics.** Dealer gamma, vol-control, 0DTE, expiries, the multi-horizon stack.
**3.4 Behavior.** Narrative formation and reversal; crowding; momentum and mean reversion as behavior; manias and meme dynamics; capitulation; the behavioral canon (loss aversion, disposition, anchoring, recency, overconfidence) applied to markets and to the operator.
**3.5 Liquidity, leverage, and cascades.** Endogenous risk; feedback loops; correlation breakdown; path dependence; forced buying and selling; short squeezes; liquidity spirals; how leverage turns a price move into a regime.
**3.6 Events and expectations.** Earnings, macro releases, policy meetings, elections, index events — the machine that sets expectations, what the options market already knows, the reaction as the object.
**3.7 Technicals** as regime, level, and trap reading.

### Layer 4 — Turning points and tails

Tops and bottoms as studied phenomena; bubbles as an anatomy with a trading playbook; crashes; the listed tails and the mutations beyond the list; the second-order scan as a standing discipline; the present read against history, kept in a dated appendix.

### Layer 5 — Craft: from research to trades

5.1 Forming a thesis (mechanism, base rate, variant perception, falsifier) · 5.2 Expression and instrument selection; payoff asymmetry · 5.3 Sizing, hedging, portfolio construction across regimes, kill switches, Kelly under uncertainty · 5.4 Execution for a small book · 5.5 Evidence and learning: the register, process versus outcome, decay detection · 5.6 Tax, accounts, wrappers, compliance · 5.7 The operator: what the best traders share, separated from the mythology; discipline; the rules.

### Layer 6 — The operating system (this operator's system, separated from the market content)

The governing doctrine; the reports as instruments and their manuals; the register; the gates; automation; and the annual structural review.

### Layer 7 — The future and the review

The standing list of what could change (your list of ~60); tripwires for each; the annual review protocol; the "what are we not thinking about" scan as a scheduled practice, not an inspiration.

## Part 3 — Structure: the proposed library

### The taxonomy: three shelves, not one list

The guide currently orders the library from the highest-level to the most specific. That is a reading order, not a taxonomy, and it hides the distinction your brief asks for. The proposal is three shelves with different roles, different update rules, and different readers:

| Shelf | Role | Update rule | Reader |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M — Markets** | How markets work: timeless mechanisms, participants, history, asset classes, behavior | Annual structural review; mechanisms rarely change, regularities re-verified, forecasts live in dated appendices | Anyone who wants to understand markets |
| **C — Craft** | How to turn understanding into decisions: edge, thesis, expression, sizing, evidence, the operator | Governed by the Doctrine; changes only at the monthly session (Doctrine Rule 20) | The operator, and anyone building a book |
| **S — System** | How *this* operator's machine works: the reports, their manuals, the register, the gates, the review | Changes with the architecture; versioned with the code | The operator and the agents that run the system |

The **Operating Doctrine** governs all three: it is the constitution the Markets papers are read through, the Craft papers implement, and the System papers execute. It is not a fourth shelf; it is the spine.

## Part 4 — Cross-cutting standards every content paper must meet

These are the changes that apply to *all* Markets-shelf papers, as opposed to the per-paper changes in Part 6. Each becomes a required chapter or convention, and the library gate (Audit 1's `tools/check_library.py`) can check for their presence by heading.

### 4.1 The Tactical Applications chapter (item 3)

Every Markets paper closes with a chapter in a fixed shape — a table, seven rows, one per horizon rung, blank rows permitted and labelled "no edge for this book at this horizon":

| Horizon | What in this paper is tradeable here | Edge type(s) | Who is on the other side, and why they transact | Why competition hasn't removed it | What would end it | Expression (instrument, structure) | Book | Report section that reads it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The nine columns are the six questions plus instrument, book, and wiring. Today the asset papers carry "Bull, Bear, and Range" cases and a position-construction section, which answer *what* to do and *with what*, but not *why the opportunity exists* or *at which horizon*. The Doctrine's Rule 11 (thesis and instrument are two decisions) and Rule 13 (no trade without a counterparty and an invalidation) are the reason this chapter exists; the chapter is the place a paper shows it has obeyed them.

Two principles the chapter must state per paper, because the brief asks the corpus to teach them and today only the Doctrine does: durable performance comes from several partially independent edges, not a model; and the small book's advantage lives in specific rows of this table (capacity-constrained, calendar-driven, horizon-flexible, do-nothing-permitted) and in no others.

### 4.2 The Wiring chapter (item 4)

Every Markets paper carries a short chapter in a fixed shape: which daily report sections read this paper's signals (§ numbers); which Weekend Synthesis items; which Monthly pillar or part; which quarterly review; which register fields it populates; which claims registry entries it owns; what the annual review re-verifies. Eleven papers have something like it today (Positioning VII, International IX, Earnings VI, Options V, Equities IX, Technicals 8, Rates V, Credit 9, Digital Assets, Metals, Currencies); nine do not (Base Rates, Tops and Bottoms, the Twenty-Five, Evidence and Inference, Volatility's is partial, Energy's partial, Foundations' is the instrument panel, Dealer's Hand's is implicit in Part IV, Portfolio Construction's is Part VII). The chapter is the boundary between Shelf M and Shelf S: the market paper says *what the signal means*; the report manual says *how it is computed and displayed*.

### 4.3 The Environments section (item 7)

Every Markets paper states, in a fixed table, how its subject behaves and how its signals read across the eighteen environments — or, where an environment is irrelevant, says so. XXVII owns the master matrix; each paper owns its row set. This is where "the last hundred years will not necessarily reflect the next twenty" becomes a discipline: the table forces the author to say what the paper's regularities assume (e.g., "credit leads equities" assumes a bank-and-bond-financed corporate sector; under fiscal dominance with financial repression the lead may vanish or invert) rather than leaving the assumption implicit.

### 4.4 The drivers-of-behavior checklist (item 11)

Each Markets paper's analytical frame must locate its subject against the drivers the brief lists: expectations versus realized, surprise, narrative formation and reversal, second- and third-order effects, crowding, positioning, reflexivity, momentum and mean reversion, liquidity cascades, leverage and deleveraging, forced flows, squeezes, manias, capitulation, tops and bottoms, volatility regimes, correlation breakdown, regime change, structural breaks, nonlinearity, path dependence, endogenous risk, feedback loops. Not every paper needs every driver; every paper needs to say which ones govern its subject and where in the corpus each is taught (XXV for expectations and reflexivity, XXX for cascades, XXVI for tops/bottoms/manias, XI for vol regimes, XVI for positioning). Two sentences the brief wants the reader to internalize — *markets trade on the difference between reality and expectations*, and *price changes behavior, positioning, fundamentals, narratives, and subsequent price* — become the opening of XXV and are cited, not restated, everywhere else.

### 4.5 Examples and commentary (item 2)

The strongest papers teach by worked example (Dealer's Hand's ledgers, Options' figures, Base Rates' "what to take from this table" blocks, Credit's five case studies). The standard becomes: every mechanism gets one worked example with numbers, one historical instance, and one "what it looked like on the day" commentary. The papers lightest on this by structure: Foundations (dispatches instead of examples), the Twenty-Five (scenario narratives instead of mechanisms in action), Positioning Parts IV–V (estimation without a worked estimate), International Parts IV–V (country surveys), Portfolio Construction (empty sections by design). The edit phase prioritizes examples there.

### 4.6 The options on-ramp (item 8)

The corpus's options content is its best region and also its steepest. Three changes make it learnable rather than merely correct: (1) reading order — own the option before you study the dealer: Options as Expression Parts I–II (the four shapes, your Greeks, the three prices) → The Dealer's Hand (the dealer's Greeks, the hedging flow, §06) → Volatility (the surface, the VRP, the regimes) → Options Parts III–V (costs, engineered payoffs, the expression table); (2) one running position — a single index put spread or collar carried through all three papers, so the reader sees the same trade from the inside, from the dealer's book, and on the surface; (3) a prerequisites note at the head of each — "this paper assumes X, taught in Y" — instead of a fourth 101. Dealer's Hand Part V (the multi-horizon stack) is the bridge from options mechanics to Book B and should be signposted as such.

### 4.7 The confidence and presentation convention (K)

*Revised 7 September 2026, with XXV Draft 1.1, and binding on every paper from here on. The original three-tier version is preserved in the paragraph below it, because the reason the markers went is part of the standard.*

**Confidence.** Unmarked text is **mechanism** — durable, argued from first principles — and carries no marker at all. An **empirical** claim keeps everything that made it checkable, the sample period, the regime it was measured in and the date it was last verified, but carries them as a short italic closing clause inside the sentence rather than as a bracketed label ahead of it. The tier name "regularity" is retired: it labelled the sentence rather than qualifying it, and a reader who has met it forty times has stopped reading it. A **forecast** is dated, low-confidence, and lives only in the dated appendix.

**Prose before tables.** Any taxonomy, list or matrix is argued in paragraphs first and summarized in a concise table afterwards. The table is a cheat sheet — fewer words than the prose it follows — and never the primary carrier of the argument. This is the standard that most changes how the closing chapters read: the nine-column Tactical Applications matrix and the eighteen-row Environments matrix are now a paragraph per horizon and a bullet per environment, with the table reduced to what a reader looks up rather than what he learns from.

**Conceptual figures.** Rounded, order-of-magnitude figures for the participant classes' ownership and turnover shares are permitted in the timeless body, provided the table is labelled "conceptual; rounded; dated figures in Appendix B" and carries one sentence on why the methodology is imperfect. The reason for the exception is that the *shape* of those two columns — that they do not rank the same way — is itself the argument, and a reader should not have to leave the chapter to see it.

**What the gate polices, unchanged.** No paragraph in a timeless body may carry a specific price, level, or probability without an as-of date. The conceptual-figures exception is a labelled band, not an exact figure, and it does not widen that rule.

*The convention this replaced.* Three tiers marked in the text: **mechanism** (durable, argued from first principles), **regularity** (empirical, with sample period, last-verified date, and the regime it was measured in), **forecast** (dated, low confidence, lives in an appendix). Base Rates and Evidence and Inference already wrote this way. It was replaced because the two markers that appeared in the body were doing different jobs: *(mechanism)* was a default asserted fifty-nine times in one paper and therefore read as noise, while *(regularity)* was carrying real information — sample, regime, date — in a bracket that invited the eye to skip it. Deleting the first and inlining the second keeps every fact the convention was built to protect and stops the reader learning to skim.

### 4.8 The small-book lens (item 12)

The Doctrine states the small-capital advantage precisely (§3.3) and its shadow. Every Tactical Applications chapter inherits that lens through the "Book" column and the "why competition hasn't removed it" column — the answer to the latter is, for most rows a small book can actually use, "capacity: the opportunity is too small for the institutions that could otherwise take it." Where that isn't the answer, the paper is claiming to out-research the institutions and should say so explicitly, because Doctrine §3.3 says that claim is usually false.

### 4.9 The Doctrine as governing paper

Three amendments, made at a monthly session per Rule 20: (1) a new Part between II and III, or a strong reference to XXV, carrying the twelve edges, the six questions, and their mapping to the four books; (2) the horizon ladder extended from four books to seven rungs, with the three rungs the books don't trade (intraday-as-such, secular, generational) stated as *inputs to stance* rather than trades; (3) a reading plan for the library (Part 3 above) and a rule that every Markets paper's Tactical Applications chapter is written under the Doctrine's rules and names them. The Doctrine remains the paper that says what this operator optimizes and refuses; the Markets papers describe the world he operates in; the two are kept apart so that the world can change without the rules changing, and the rules can change without rewriting the world.

## Part 5 — The Annual Structural Review (items 5 and 6)

The papers are meant to be timeless; the plumbing, the participants, the relationships, and the technology are not. Today the review exists as a "next edition" note in three papers and a mutation scan in one. The proposal makes it a mechanism with four instruments, a schedule, a log, and tripwires — written up as paper XXXII on the System shelf.

### 5.1 Four instruments

**Instrument 1 — The Assumptions and Falsifiers appendix (every Markets paper).** A fixed-format appendix listing: (a) the mechanisms the paper treats as durable; (b) every empirical regularity it relies on, with the sample period, the regime it was measured in, and the date last verified; (c) for each, the falsifier — the observation that would mean the section is wrong; (d) the "what could change" items from the standing register (5.3) that touch this paper; (e) an inventory of every dated figure in the paper's appendices with the series it can be recomputed from. This appendix is what the review reads; without it the review is an essay.

**Instrument 2 — The dated-figure recomputation (automated).** Every figure in a paper's dated appendices is recomputed annually from the point-in-time store with `available_at`, stored as an observation, and diffed against the published value; moves beyond a stated tolerance are flagged. Base Rates already specifies this for itself; it becomes the rule for all appendices. This is a `make annual-review` target, not a session.

**Instrument 3 — The relationship health check (semi-automated).** For each regularity in every paper's appendix (b), a rolling test on the most recent window against the sample it was measured in: does credit still lead equities and by how long; does curve inversion still precede recession; has the gold–real-rates beta re-attached or stayed broken; does the VIX–realized spread still carry the premium; does the yen–vol link still hold; does the closing-auction imbalance still predict the next open. Each returns *holding / weakening / broken / re-attached* with the numbers. Broken regularities are the review's first agenda item, because they are where the papers are now teaching something false.

**Instrument 4 — The structural scan (a session, on a fixed questionnaire).** Answered once a year for the corpus and once for each paper, in writing, in the review log:

- The adversarial questions from your brief, verbatim: what if the prevailing explanation is wrong; what if a relationship thought stable stops working; what if a major participant changes behavior; what if technology changes who provides liquidity or information; what if a currently insignificant asset class becomes systemic; what if the next crisis resembles none we study; what if a source of alpha disappears; where might new alpha emerge; what is too small to notice today; what does the corpus assume only because today's system makes it look natural.
- The "what are we not thinking about" pass, with three forced inputs so it is not an inspiration exercise: (i) the Twenty-Five's mutation scan, re-run against the year's events (Part 3's ten mutations become a living list with promotion/retirement); (ii) a size scan — any market, structure, or participant class that crossed a stated threshold this year (a notional, a share of volume, a share of ownership) is a candidate subject; (iii) the register's own anomalies — trades that worked or failed for reasons no paper explains are the library's most honest gap detector, and the Systematic Book already records them.
- The per-paper decision, per section: **reaffirm / amend / rewrite / retire**, with the reason, and the version bump on the masthead (the masthead owns the version; the guide copies it).

### 5.2 Schedule

- **Annual, January:** Instruments 2 and 3 run; Instrument 4 is a two-session review (corpus first, then papers ranked by number of broken or weakening regularities); the log is written; the guide's status column is updated (last reviewed, next due, health).
- **Off-cycle, on a tripwire:** any item in the standing register (5.3) whose indicator crosses its threshold triggers the review of the papers it touches, within the month. The Doctrine's Rule 20 (changes only at the monthly session) is preserved: the tripwire opens the question; the monthly session decides.
- **Gate:** `library-check` warns when any paper's last-reviewed date is more than thirteen months old, and fails when a paper's appendix (b) is missing.

### I · Foundations (Disruptive Themes) — 21,180 words
